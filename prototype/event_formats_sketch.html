Event formats - six sketches against the shipped card

separate prototype · real CSS and art from grimtoll_slice.html · real card text from clash / toll / pedlar / coin · the voice lines are new and not canon · the strip and the sigils were cut on 2026-08-14
SHIPPED, BUILD 8f.184 · 620px · THE BASELINE

WHAT IT IS

The container every one of the 34 road cards uses: title, letterbox painting, a block of narrator prose, the doors, then an outcome block with chips.

WHY IT READS AS "TEXT"

Nothing here is badly written and nothing is too long. The mean card is 107 words, the ceiling is 175, and Battle Brothers runs several times that. The problem is that all 34 cards have the same rhythm, and there is no face anywhere: three paragraphs arrive at once, from nobody in particular, about people you cannot see.

WHAT THE SKETCHES CHANGE

None of them adds words. A gives the words a mouth. E gives them somebody to argue with. C gives them a geography. H throws the paragraph away and makes the choice the screen. B gives them a stack.

G is not a container at all. It hands the whole card to one person, who may be wrong about it.

A · THE SPEAKER (Hades) · CLICK THE CARD TO ADVANCE

THE SHAPE STOLEN

Hades: a bust at the edge of the frame, a name, one line at a time, and the player taps the pace. Hades is not short. It has hundreds of thousands of words. It reads fast because no line is ever waiting for you to finish another one.

WHAT CHANGES HERE

The two count sentences that used to be narrator furniture are now spoken by the two people with family on each side. Skree counts his cousins. Bruht counts his. Same information, and now it costs nothing to read because somebody wants to tell you.

The painting stops being a letterbox on top of prose and becomes the ground the speaker stands in.

WHAT IT COSTS

free the beats already exist: every body is written with \n\n breaks, so the split is a .split, not a rewrite.

free portraits, cast(), {TELLER} and {OGRE}/{RATKIN} all exist. 44 token uses in the build already.

writing one or two voice lines per card, 34 cards.

clicks three taps where there was one. Gate it to HEAVY cards, and let any click reveal the rest.

B · THE COLUMN (Esoteric Ebb / Disco Elysium) · LINES ACCUMULATE

THE SHAPE STOLEN

Disco and its descendants: every line is owned by a named voice, the column keeps what was already said, and the choice list at the foot shows the doors you cannot open together with the reason.

THE TWO DEVICES WORTH TAKING

The narrator is a character. Calling it THE ROAD costs one string and turns an omniscient block into one voice among several.

A locked door is shown, not hidden. Today need and needRace filter doors out of existence, so a player never learns that a ratkin in the company would have opened something. Showing it locked turns a hidden gate into a reason to recruit differently.

THE TRAIT VOICE

The blue line is the Disco skill-check trick, and it is cheaper than it looks: every person already carries a trait (stubborn, twitchy, big, nofear, windtouched). A trait voice is a lookup on data that exists, not a new system. It is still the most expensive thing on this page, because it wants a line per trait per card.

WHAT IT COSTS

renderer a new card body. Real work, but it is one renderer for all 34.

scroll a stacking column can outgrow the box. The shipped card already solved this once: only the prose scrolls, the doors stay pinned.

C · THE STANDOFF (Banner Saga) · 880px · THE DOORS SIT WHERE THE PEOPLE ARE

THE SHAPE STOLEN

Banner Saga: a wide painted frame, one party on each side facing the other, a band of text beneath, and the choice list under that.

THE ONE IDEA THAT MATTERS

The doors are laid out where the people are. Help the ratkin is on the left because the ratkin are on the left. Help the ogres is on the right. The cruel door is underneath both, which is exactly where you are standing while you wait for them to finish.

That is a choice you can make before reading a word, which is the same goal the intent glyphs had in #154, arriving through layout instead of through a symbol.

AND IT SHOWS THE FIGHT

Four spears against three clubs, drawn as four bodies against three. The receipt for a battle door is a force comparison, and right now that comparison is a sentence.

WHAT IT COSTS

free race portraits exist (RACEPORTRAIT), and the enemy composition is already counted, not remembered: clashFoes is the source of truth.

width 880px against the card's 620. It wants to be a full-screen scene, not a dialog.

narrow only fits cards with two visible sides. That is clash, the wedding, the tax-man, the clan cart. Maybe six of 34.

E · THE TWO-HANDER (Hades, the other shape) · CLICK TO ADVANCE

THE SHAPE STOLEN

Hades again, but the conversation layout rather than the monologue one: one face on the left, one on the right, and the frame belongs to whoever is talking.

THE WHOLE DEVICE IS ONE CSS LINE

The non-speaker dims and steps back. Without it two portraits read as a group photo and the player has to find the name plate to know who said the line. With it, you know before you read. It is grayscale(.85) brightness(.32) and an 8px nudge.

WHAT IT IS FOR

The cards where somebody talks back: the pedlar, the tax-man, the toll bridge, the clan cart. Not the ones where the company looks at a thing.

Note the last beat. The pedlar is friendly for two lines, and then the narrator says three words and they land as a threat. That rhythm is only available once the voices are separated.

THE TOGGLE UNDER THE CARD

Doors as actions is what ships today. Doors as lines is the expensive version: the choice becomes something the Captain says, which is the strongest form of this shape and the most writing.

94 doors rewriting every label as speech is a large job, and speech is worse than an action label on any card where nobody is listening.

art a talking card wants a face for the person talking. The pedlar here is wearing the generic human portrait, which is the game's own fallback.

J · THE STAGE · YOUR PERSON LEFT, THE SUBJECT RIGHT, THE DIALOGUE DROPS IN · CLICK TO ADVANCE

YOUR SHAPE, BUILT

Three layers. The painting is the where, pushed back. Left is somebody from the company, context, so it stays in the gloom. Right is what the choice is about. The dialogue drops in over all of it.

THE RIGHT-HAND SLOT COSTS NO NEW ART

⚑ It is the map sight. The exact painting the player was looking at on the road one second before the card opened, at 216px instead of 96px. The ogre on his stone, the drowned chapel, the collector.

There are 31 of them already embedded in MAP_ART, they are PNG cut-outs with real alpha, so they need no frame and no mask. That is the difference between them and the portraits, and it is why the right side looks clean while the left had to be faded into the dark.

It also does something no other sketch here does: it carries the map into the card. The thing you decided to walk to is the thing now standing in front of you.

THE FLANKS ARE THE SPEAKER INDICATOR

They are not decoration. Whoever is talking lights up, and everything else drops back. That is what lets the plate stay small and lets the picture be most of the screen.

⛔ Three states, not two, and that was a real bug caught by driving the second scene. The first cut lit both flanks at once on the chapel, because one highlight was being asked to mean SPEAKING on one card and SUBJECT on the other. Now: idle is present and uninvolved, subject is what the card is about and never moves, lit is who is speaking and moves every beat.

Watch the sitting stone: the stone is lit while the ogre asks his three questions, then Bruht lights on the left when your own ogre offers to take over, which is the card's needRace:'ogre' door arriving in the fiction before it arrives as a button.

IT ALSO HANDLES "OR AN EVENT"

Switch to the chapel. Nobody talks back: the right slot holds a place and stays lit throughout as the subject. Same container, no special case.

WHAT IT COSTS

free map sights, portraits, beats and cast() all exist.

width 940px. This is a full-stage screen, not a 620px dialog, and that is the real decision in it.

no sight ⛔ five nodes have no map art on purpose (cache, armour, and the abstract type marks). The right slot needs a documented fallback before this ships, or those cards open with a hole in them.

I · THE PLATE (Hades, the actual dialogue box) · SAME BEATS AS E, COMPARE THEM

THIS IS THE ONE YOU MEANT

Hades does not paste a cut-out over the art. It puts a small framed medallion on a plate, and the painting behind stays whole.

It runs the same four beats as E, same words, same pedlar. The only thing that changed is how the speaker is drawn. Flip between the two tabs.

WHY A AND E LOOKED PASTED, AND IT IS NOT TASTE

⛔ The build's portraits are square JPEG heads with backgrounds. There is no alpha channel to cut, so masking one into a bust always leaves that soft rectangular edge. It was never going to look right.

A ring turns the same file into furniture. The frame does the job the alpha channel cannot, and it needs no new art at all: one border-radius, three stacked rings in a box-shadow, and an inset shadow to seat the head in it.

THE DEVICE IT REPLACES

E dimmed the silent bust to say who was talking. Here the ring warms instead: gold for the company, bronze for whoever is not, dull iron with a rune when it is the narrator. One property, no second portrait needed, and it works when there are five speakers rather than two.

WHAT IT COSTS

free no art, no cut-outs, no new files. Every portrait in the build already fits this frame.

free the painting is never covered, which is the other half of the complaint: the event art is the most expensive thing on the card and A and E were both standing on it.

ornament the corner brackets are two CSS pseudo-elements. A real frame would be drawn, and that is the only art this shape ever wants.

H · THE OFFER (Hades, the boon pick) · THE DOORS ARE THE SCREEN

THE SHAPE STOLEN

The third Hades screen, and the one that is not a text screen at all. When Hades offers you a boon it does not narrate: it lays two or three cards side by side, each with a medallion, a name, one line of effect, and lets the choosing be the whole interaction.

WHY IT BELONGS HERE MORE THAN IT LOOKS

Every part of it already exists on a RabbleBound door. The medallion is the intent glyph from #154. The effect line is the c: sub-line. The rarity colour is danger:true. Nothing new is invented, the pieces are just given room.

And it answers the complaint directly: the prose drops to one line, and what the player spends their attention on is the decision instead of the paragraph.

THE TOGGLE, AND WHY IT IS THE REAL TEST

Hades always offers three. Style rule 4 permits four. Switch to the toll-man above and watch the cards get narrow: that is the stress case, and it is where this layout has to prove it is honest rather than pretty.

WHAT IT COSTS AND WHAT IT LOSES

free glyphs, sub-lines and the danger flag are all shipped fields.

width it wants a wide stage, not a 620px dialog.

the prose ⛔ this is the one shape on the page that cuts writing rather than reframing it. A 140-word heavy card does not fit here and must not be squeezed in. It is a container for the cards that were always a choice between three things, and using it everywhere would delete the road's voice to make a menu.

G · HIS OWN STORY · SWITCH THE TELLER

WHAT IT IS

Your line: "tell it as the story of one of the characters, I found a coin". The card is one person's account, in the first person, present tense.

THE THING IT BUYS THAT NOTHING ELSE HERE DOES

The teller can be wrong. A narrator who says "four ratkin with spears" is stating a fact. A frightened ratkin who says "four spears, maybe five" is showing you who he is, and the count becomes characterisation.

So the split is clean and worth writing down: the prose is his account, the door sub-lines stay the game's exact figures. Style rule 8 is untouched, and the unreliable half is free.

WHY IT IS CHEAPER THAN IT LOOKS

free this card already picks a teller. openEvent casts {TELLER} for coin and star, and the shipped line is literally "{TELLER} picks it up".

Switch the teller above and read the three. Same event, three companies, three different cards, and the difference is the person, not the writing.

WHY IT IS NOT THE DEFAULT

voice first person fights rule 1, the BG3 narrator voice, which is the house style for all 34 cards. Used on four or five cards it is a change of rhythm. Used on all of them it is a different game.

per teller a card written this way wants a version per possible teller, or it wants a teller it can rely on.

Which format for which card

Nothing says one container has to serve all 34. The build already sorts cards three ways it could reuse: the length band (light 45-90, medium 90-140, heavy 140-175), whether a door starts a battle, and whether the card has one live door or several.

A, E and I are one format wearing three treatments

This is the thing that came out of building them side by side. A is E with one bust, and I is the same beats with the bust replaced by a framed medallion. Same renderer, same beat walk, same door footer. The real decision was never which of the three: it is whether the road uses a beat-walked card with a named speaker, and then how that speaker is drawn.

So if A felt uncertain, that instinct was reading a production problem rather than a layout one. The portraits in this build are square JPEG heads with backgrounds. There is no alpha to cut, so any bust treatment leaves the pasted edge that A and E both have. I solves it by not cutting anything, and the frame turns out to be what the missing alpha channel was for.

The recommendation, in order

1. Build I. One container for the road, medallion treatment, painting never covered. The beats are already marked with \n\n, cast() already picks who speaks, and the portraits finally fit their container. It is the only shape here that improves every card rather than a category of them.

2. C, narrow, six cards. It is the best-looking screen on this page and it is a special case, not a system. Build it after I so it inherits the same door footer.

3. H on a handful. Cheap once I exists, because it reuses the same door fields. Never let it spread: it is the one shape that deletes prose instead of reframing it.

4. G as a writing experiment, on one card, before any of the above. See the box below.

5. B held. Its home is the camps, and the camps are not the complaint.

A and E stay on the page as the rejected treatment rather than as options. They are worth keeping open beside I for one reason: they show what the same four beats look like when the speaker is standing on the painting instead of framed beside it.

The test to run before any of it

Every format on this page is a bet on one idea: that attributed lines read better than narrated ones. The container is the expensive half of that bet and the writing is the load-bearing half, so the writing should be tested first, and it needs no code at all.

Take three cards. Rewrite each so one or two lines belong to somebody in the company. Read the six versions in a text file. If the attributed versions are not obviously better as plain text, no amount of busts and dimming will rescue them, and the answer is somewhere else entirely.

G is that test with the smallest possible surface: one card, three tellers, already cast by the game. It is on this page precisely because it can be judged without building anything.

What none of these fix

The measurement I would take before any of it: how many of the 34 cards change a single word if you swap the party? Attribution only pays if the speaker is actually different from run to run. If the answer is small, the first job is the {ANY} and {OGRE} tokens, not the renderer.
