Event formats - seven sketches against the shipped card

separate prototype · real CSS and art from grimtoll_slice.html · real card text from clash / toll / pedlar / coin · the voice lines are new and not canon
SHIPPED, BUILD 8f.184 · 620px · THE BASELINE

WHAT IT IS

The container every one of the 34 road cards uses: title, letterbox painting, a block of narrator prose, the doors, then an outcome block with chips.

WHY IT READS AS "TEXT"

Nothing here is badly written and nothing is too long. The mean card is 107 words, the ceiling is 175, and Battle Brothers runs several times that. The problem is that all 34 cards have the same rhythm, and there is no face anywhere: three paragraphs arrive at once, from nobody in particular, about people you cannot see.

WHAT THE SEVEN SKETCHES CHANGE

None of them adds words. A gives the words a mouth. B gives them a stack. C gives them a geography. D takes the screen away entirely. E gives them somebody to argue with.

The last two are not containers. F puts a mark where a fact used to be a sentence. G hands the whole card to one person, who may be wrong about it.

A · THE SPEAKER (Hades) · CLICK THE CARD TO ADVANCE

THE SHAPE STOLEN

Hades: a bust at the edge of the frame, a name, one line at a time, and the player taps the pace. Hades is not short. It has hundreds of thousands of words. It reads fast because no line is ever waiting for you to finish another one.

WHAT CHANGES HERE

The two count sentences that used to be narrator furniture are now spoken by the two people with family on each side. Skree counts his cousins. Bruht counts his. Same information, and now it costs nothing to read because somebody wants to tell you.

The painting stops being a letterbox on top of prose and becomes the ground the speaker stands in.

WHAT IT COSTS

free the beats already exist: every body is written with \n\n breaks, so the split is a .split, not a rewrite.

free portraits, cast(), {TELLER} and {OGRE}/{RATKIN} all exist. 44 token uses in the build already.

writing one or two voice lines per card, 34 cards.

clicks three taps where there was one. Gate it to HEAVY cards, and let any click reveal the rest.

B · THE COLUMN (Esoteric Ebb / Disco Elysium) · LINES ACCUMULATE

THE SHAPE STOLEN

Disco and its descendants: every line is owned by a named voice, the column keeps what was already said, and the choice list at the foot shows the doors you cannot open together with the reason.

THE TWO DEVICES WORTH TAKING

The narrator is a character. Calling it THE ROAD costs one string and turns an omniscient block into one voice among several.

A locked door is shown, not hidden. Today need and needRace filter doors out of existence, so a player never learns that a ratkin in the company would have opened something. Showing it locked turns a hidden gate into a reason to recruit differently.

THE TRAIT VOICE

The blue line is the Disco skill-check trick, and it is cheaper than it looks: every person already carries a trait (stubborn, twitchy, big, nofear, windtouched). A trait voice is a lookup on data that exists, not a new system. It is still the most expensive thing on this page, because it wants a line per trait per card.

WHAT IT COSTS

renderer a new card body. Real work, but it is one renderer for all 34.

scroll a stacking column can outgrow the box. The shipped card already solved this once: only the prose scrolls, the doors stay pinned.

C · THE STANDOFF (Banner Saga) · 880px · THE DOORS SIT WHERE THE PEOPLE ARE

THE SHAPE STOLEN

Banner Saga: a wide painted frame, one party on each side facing the other, a band of text beneath, and the choice list under that.

THE ONE IDEA THAT MATTERS

The doors are laid out where the people are. Help the ratkin is on the left because the ratkin are on the left. Help the ogres is on the right. The cruel door is underneath both, which is exactly where you are standing while you wait for them to finish.

That is a choice you can make before reading a word, which is the same goal the intent glyphs had in #154, arriving through layout instead of through a symbol.

AND IT SHOWS THE FIGHT

Four spears against three clubs, drawn as four bodies against three. The receipt for a battle door is a force comparison, and right now that comparison is a sentence.

WHAT IT COSTS

free race portraits exist (RACEPORTRAIT), and the enemy composition is already counted, not remembered: clashFoes is the source of truth.

width 880px against the card's 620. It wants to be a full-screen scene, not a dialog.

narrow only fits cards with two visible sides. That is clash, the wedding, the tax-man, the clan cart. Maybe six of 34.

D · THE STRIP · A PICKUP NEVER OPENS A SCREEN

WHAT IT IS

The only sketch here that is a cut rather than an addition, which makes it the only one that fits the clarity pass without asking for an exception.

A one-door pickup has no decision to protect. The rules already say it wears its loot on the card instead of hiding it. This goes one step further: it does not get a card at all. It happens on the road, the company keeps walking, and one button dismisses it.

WHY IT MAKES THE OTHER THREE BETTER

14 of 34 road cards are LIGHT. If a third of the deck stops taking over the screen, the full card stops being the thing that always happens and becomes the thing that happens when it matters. Rhythm is made by contrast, and right now there is none.

WHAT IT COSTS

free the gate exists: visible.length===1 && !visible[0].battle, computed after the need filters.

small a road-level strip and its dismiss.

loss a light card loses its painting at full size. Some of those paintings are good.

E · THE TWO-HANDER (Hades, the other shape) · CLICK TO ADVANCE

THE SHAPE STOLEN

Hades again, but the conversation layout rather than the monologue one: one face on the left, one on the right, and the frame belongs to whoever is talking.

THE WHOLE DEVICE IS ONE CSS LINE

The non-speaker dims and steps back. Without it two portraits read as a group photo and the player has to find the name plate to know who said the line. With it, you know before you read. It is grayscale(.85) brightness(.32) and an 8px nudge.

WHAT IT IS FOR

The cards where somebody talks back: the pedlar, the tax-man, the toll bridge, the clan cart. Not the ones where the company looks at a thing.

Note the last beat. The pedlar is friendly for two lines, and then the narrator says three words and they land as a threat. That rhythm is only available once the voices are separated.

THE TOGGLE UNDER THE CARD

Doors as actions is what ships today. Doors as lines is the expensive version: the choice becomes something the Captain says, which is the strongest form of this shape and the most writing.

94 doors rewriting every label as speech is a large job, and speech is worse than an action label on any card where nobody is listening.

art a talking card wants a face for the person talking. The pedlar here is wearing the generic human portrait, which is the game's own fallback.

F · THE SIGIL LINE · CLICK A MARK TO OPEN IT

WHAT IT IS

The card readable at two depths. A band of marks states the facts for the glance. The prose underneath is there for whoever wants it, and it no longer has to carry the information, so it can be mood instead of report.

The player picks the depth. That is the honest version of "make reading more interesting": some nights you read the card, some nights you read the marks.

THE RULE THAT MAKES IT A LANGUAGE

A mark used once is decoration. A mark used on six cards is a language. The strip at the foot is the whole point: the same ⚑ on the toll, the cart and the wedding teaches the Snare Clan without a codex entry.

The build already has 11 marks that work this way, from CHOICE_ICO and RACE_ICO.

WHERE THIS GOES WRONG

receipt ⛔ a mark that says A PURSE is one step from promising an amount. The rule holds: marks are intent, the chips are the receipt, and no number ever appears in both.

one meaning ⛔ 💀 is already the lowest morale face. A symbol may not mean two things on one screen, so a real set has to be audited against the mood ladder and the resource icons before a single new one is drawn.

art emoji will not carry a house language. A real set is drawn, and that is the largest art job on this page.

G · HIS OWN STORY · SWITCH THE TELLER

WHAT IT IS

Your line: "tell it as the story of one of the characters, I found a coin". The card is one person's account, in the first person, present tense.

THE THING IT BUYS THAT NOTHING ELSE HERE DOES

The teller can be wrong. A narrator who says "four ratkin with spears" is stating a fact. A frightened ratkin who says "four spears, maybe five" is showing you who he is, and the count becomes characterisation.

So the split is clean and worth writing down: the prose is his account, the door sub-lines stay the game's exact figures. Style rule 8 is untouched, and the unreliable half is free.

WHY IT IS CHEAPER THAN IT LOOKS

free this card already picks a teller. openEvent casts {TELLER} for coin and star, and the shipped line is literally "{TELLER} picks it up".

Switch the teller above and read the three. Same event, three companies, three different cards, and the difference is the person, not the writing.

WHY IT IS NOT THE DEFAULT

voice first person fights rule 1, the BG3 narrator voice, which is the house style for all 34 cards. Used on four or five cards it is a change of rhythm. Used on all of them it is a different game.

per teller a card written this way wants a version per possible teller, or it wants a teller it can rely on.

Which format for which card

Nothing says one container has to serve all 34. The build already sorts cards three ways it could reuse: the length band (light 45-90, medium 90-140, heavy 140-175), whether a door starts a battle, and whether the card has one live door or several.

Two of these are not containers at all

F is a layer and G is a voice. Neither competes with A through E: the sigil band can sit on top of the speaker card, and the first-person story can be told inside the strip. That is why the table above gives them a row and not a slot. It also means they can be tried on one card without deciding anything.

If only one of them gets built

D and A, as one entry. D is a cut and pays for itself immediately: a third of the deck stops stopping the game. A is the cheapest per-card gain in the set, because the beats and the cast system and the portraits are all already in the build, and it is the one that answers the actual complaint, which is that reading is not interesting rather than that reading is long.

E is A with a second bust, so if A is built, E costs one more portrait slot and a dim class. Those two should be one entry, not two.

C is the one to build second and narrow: six cards, and it will look better in a screenshot than anything else here.

G is the cheapest test on the whole page. It is one card, rewritten three times, and it is already cast. If the coin told by Skree reads better than the coin told by nobody, that answers the question this page was opened to ask.

B is the biggest and the one I would hold. It wants a locked-door convention, a trait-voice table and a new scrolling body, and its natural home is the camps rather than the road.

F I would not start from the art. Audit the marks that already exist first, against the mood faces and the resource icons, because the set has to be collision-free before it is beautiful.

What none of these fix

The measurement I would take before any of it: how many of the 34 cards change a single word if you swap the party? Attribution only pays if the speaker is actually different from run to run. If the answer is small, the first job is the {ANY} and {OGRE} tokens, not the renderer.
