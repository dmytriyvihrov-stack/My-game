# Sanya - playtest report

## Verdict
GRIMTOLL is a hex tactics game about grubby medieval mercenaries where the fight is as much about people's *nerve* as their hit points — you can win by frightening a man out of the room instead of killing him, and bystanders will join in once you look like you're winning. The writing is genuinely good and it teaches by doing things to you rather than by lecturing. But the moment-to-moment clicking got unreliable: I had six clicks in a row do nothing with zero explanation, and I never found the campaign layer the front menu promised.
**Would I play again: yes** — one reason: the nerve/morale system is a real idea and I want to see what a whole company of these people feels like on the road.

## Where I stopped and why
**Stop rule 1: I would stop.** Concrete moment: turn IX of the prologue fight, my captain at 23/57 HP and 0 armour, two broken enemies fleeing around the far side of a tavern. I selected MOVE, clicked the hex right next to him — nothing happened. Sixth dead click in a row (two attacks out of range, one blocked move, three more). No error text, no flash, no sound. I couldn't move, couldn't shoot, and there is no reachable menu to leave a battle (the "☰ MENU" button only renders while a tutorial card is dimming the HUD). That's the point where on my own couch I close the laptop.

High point: "THE ROOM IS WATCHING — strangers have been watching this from the benches... most would rather watch an unfairness than join a fight that already looks lost." I actually said "oh, that's cool" out loud.
Low point: the last ten minutes of the prologue — a chase around a tavern where half my clicks did nothing.

## Where I got stuck or confused
1. **Attacking out of reach, silently.** Trying to hit the drunk two hexes away with ARMING SWORD (REACH 1). Expected: an "out of reach" nudge, or the target simply not being clickable. What happened: nothing at all. Took me one wasted click and a screenshot to work out I had to move first. Fix: any word on screen. Even "too far".
2. **Ranged range is invisible.** Archer's HUNTING BOW and the mage's EMBER. Expected: a lit ring of hexes I can shoot. What happened: I clicked enemies three times and nothing happened. It took me ~15 actions to discover that the tooltip *does* say "1 ACT · RANGE 5", but it renders in the middle of the screen where I wasn't looking, and the card face only shows the damage ("14-21"), while MOVE's card face shows "4 HEXES". Never got a shot off with a ranged unit all session. Fix: put reach on the card face like you do for move, and light up the reachable hexes.
3. **Which hexes can I walk on?** The tutorial says "click a green hex to walk". There are no green hexes. Walkable ground is a slightly brighter shade of the same teal, over a busy pixel-art tavern floor with tables and bodies on it. I could not reliably tell walkable from blocked, and blocked moves also fail silently. Never solved it; it's what ended my session.
4. **No menu, no escape.** Pressed Escape expecting a pause menu — nothing (Escape is "cancel", which I only learned later from the rules page). The "☰ MENU" button at bottom-left is drawn *underneath* the character panel and only becomes visible when a tutorial card dims the HUD. So once I wanted to leave the prologue and go try "Take the road", I couldn't. Never solved.
5. **What just hit me?** Twice I ended a turn and came back with armour 20 → 4 and HP 43 → 27. The enemy turn resolves fast and I get no summary. I know there's a BATTLELOG button but I shouldn't have to open a log to know who hit me. Never really solved.
6. **The "tutorial fight" is a prologue.** I was 45 actions in, enemies still spawning, and I nearly bailed — then a card told me the strangers who joined "will still be walking with you". So it's the recruitment prologue and the length is deliberate. Just call it Prologue on the menu; I almost quit out of it before the payoff.

## What I understood, and what I did not
- **Movement** — PARTLY. 4 hexes for 1 action, and moving one hex leaves you 3 more on the second move ("MOVEMENT 4, then 3" on the hover card). Understood. But I could not read the terrain, so in practice I often couldn't move where I wanted.
- **Attacking / actions per turn** — UNDERSTOOD. Two actions a turn, diamonds under each skill show cost, the strong ones cost two. Hit % and damage float over the target, and hovering a target with an attack selected breaks it down: "CHANCE TO HIT 58% / HITPOINT DAMAGE 18–26 / FRONT / 😐 +1 / Reach 1".
- **Armour vs hitpoints** — UNDERSTOOD. "Iron soaks a blow before flesh does. The grey bar is the harness; the red one under it is the man inside." Damage popped as "−5 arm" then "−12". Clear.
- **Nerve / morale** — UNDERSTOOD, and it's the best thing here. It moves in combat: being hit dropped me HAPPY → OK and my hit chance 66% → 61%; a kill took me to IT ROCKS; empty nerve = BROKEN = the man runs for the door; even a *miss* shaves an enemy's nerve. The rules page confirmed the model ("The rung they started on is a magnet... the top rung is only ever bought with a kill, an order or a rally").
- **Enemy behaviour** — PARTLY. I know what each enemy *can* do (the hover card's "WHAT IT CAN DO TO YOU" list is excellent) and the red-tinted hexes show where they can reach. I never know what any of them is *about to* do.
- **Flanking / facing** — PARTLY. Told to me in a card ("A flank is easier to hit; the small of the back is easier still"), and the rules page says back arc is +30 to hit and a quarter more damage and is drawn in red under the body. I never actually managed to get behind anyone.
- **Zone of control / disengage** — UNDERSTOOD. Walking out of a fight gives free swings; DISENGAGE costs an action and *is* your step. Taught cleanly and the skill appears contextually.
- **Classes / races** — PARTLY. Captain, spearwoman, battle-mage, archer, an ogre — each with distinct skills (SPEAR WALL, IRON-OATH, CRIPPLING SHOT). I met them; I never got to use most of them properly.
- **Turn order** — PARTLY. END TURN passes to the next of my people, not the whole round; the rules say initiative is shown along the top, but the row of tiny portraits down the left edge was too small for me to read who had acted.
- **Money, wages, food, gear slots, days on the road, events, resting, contracts** — NO. I never reached them. The main menu's "Take the road" is presumably where they live and I couldn't get out of the prologue to try it. Given the tagline is "YOU CANNOT AFFORD TO BE GOOD TO EVERYONE", that's the whole promise of the game and I saw none of it.

## The fights
Melee felt genuinely good and in-control: I knew my chance, my damage, what the armour would eat, and why the number was what it was. The nerve layer gave every exchange a second axis — I was watching a man's face go 🙂 → 😐 → 😕 → 💀 and that mattered more than his HP bar. The moment my whole squad's morale ticked up together off one kill was the best beat in the session.

Ranged was the opposite: I never landed a single arrow or spell because I could not tell what was in range, and clicks failed silently. That half of the game was pure clicking and hoping.

Did I know what enemies could do before they did it? Their *capabilities*, yes, beautifully. Their *intentions*, no — and I felt that absence hard, because Into the Breach spoiled me. I'd end a turn and lose 16 armour and 16 HP without ever having been shown it coming. Nothing felt unfair exactly (55–63% is 55–63%, and two misses in a row is just dice), but the fights sag: rabble have 40+ HP and 5 armour, my sword does 16–24 at ~58%, so one no-name drunk is six or seven coin-flips. And broken enemies run to the far wall, recover their nerve, and come back, which turned the end of the prologue into a chase around furniture.

One real annoyance: the weapon doesn't stay selected. Every single swing is two clicks — pick sword, click man, pick sword, click man. Over a long fight that's hundreds of extra clicks.

## Reading and looking
The amount of text is mostly right and the *quality* is high — short, dry, specific. "He is full of ale. Slower to land a blow, easier to catch with one." "Widowed thrice." "On this island, most would rather watch an unfairness than join a fight that already looks lost." I read those properly instead of skimming, which almost never happens to me.

Problems:
- Most of the HUD is 10–11px. The skill card labels, the stat numbers, the "68% / 13%" pair at the bottom left (I still don't know for certain those are skill and dodge — I inferred it from the hover card saying "SKILL / DODGE 68 / 13"). I had to lean in constantly.
- The row of party portraits down the far-left edge is ~35px tall with no names — I could never tell who was who or who had acted.
- The "? RULES" page is the best-written thing in the build and it's behind a 10px link. Its layout is also broken: bold phrases are torn out of their sentences and floated elsewhere, so I read "Standing inside an enemy's reach puts you in its ." and had to reassemble it.
- The walkable-hex highlight is almost invisible against the tavern floor art.
- The "☰ MENU" button is drawn under the character panel.

## Three things I would fix first
1. **Never let a click do nothing silently.** Out of range, blocked, no line of fire — say so, in one word, where I'm looking. This single thing caused most of my frustration and it's what ended my session.
2. **Show me range and reach on the board.** Light the hexes an attack can reach the moment I select it (and put "REACH 1" / "RANGE 5" on the card face next to the damage, the way MOVE shows "4 HEXES"). Make the walkable-hex tint actually readable over the floor art.
3. **Give me a pause menu on Escape, and make ☰ MENU visible.** I should always be able to leave a fight and go try another mode. Related: let the selected weapon stay selected between swings.

## Something I liked
The nerve system, and specifically the moment Vesna speared a man dead and four little "◡ ▲" arrows floated up over my other people at the same time. I understood instantly, without being told, that killing well makes my whole company braver — and that the reverse is coming for me later. That plus "the room is watching, and it joins the side that's winning" is a genuinely fresh idea about what a fight *is*, and it's the reason I'd come back to this once the clicks work.
