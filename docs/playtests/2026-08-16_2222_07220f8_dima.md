# Dima - playtest report

## Verdict
GRIMTOLL is a hex-and-two-actions mercenary tactics game with a morale engine at its centre and a road layer with four currencies and a wage clock. The fights are the good part: nerve is not a flavour bar, it feeds dodge, it cascades when somebody dies, and the whole cascade is legible on the field without opening a menu. The road is thinner — beautiful, well written, but it only asked me a real question once, and when it did it was excellent. Would I play it again: **yes**, and the one reason is that killing the right man visibly makes every other enemy easier to kill, which is a loop I want to keep pulling.

## Where I stopped and why
**Rule 1 — I would stop.** Not from frustration; from having got what I came for. I was in the second fight, three ogres at 112 HP each with the trait "Does not run", i.e. the morale engine I'd just learned to exploit is switched off for them. That's a good design and I respected it, but I could see it was a twenty-minute grind and I already had the shape of both layers. The concrete moment: I opened the BATTLELOG, read "Nothing heals here — only days on the road do that", realised that line tied the two layers together and that nobody had shown it to me until turn six of the second battle, and decided I'd rather write that down than swing another forty times.

## Where I got stuck or confused

1. **Silent no-op clicks on hexes.** I was trying to disengage to the hearth corner. I expected the click to either move me or tell me why not. It did nothing at all — no cursor change, no flash, no message — twice in a row, because I'd clicked pixels between hex centres. Cost me two actions and a turn of tempo. Never fully solved; I worked around it by clicking only refs the interface enumerated. **Fix:** highlight the hex under the pointer, or dim/redden it when unreachable.

2. **Movement range vs enemy threat vs floor tint.** All three are flat colour washes on the same hexes and they overlap. In the 1v3 turn I genuinely could not tell "I may stand here" from "he can hit me here". Never solved. **Fix:** outline for reach, fill for threat, or a toggle key.

3. **The ogre's inspect panel doesn't open on hover.** I hovered the sprite at chest, at feet, and at the hex below, three separate times across two fights, and got nothing. Human-sized sprites (73x99) answer instantly; the 106x117 ogre does not. I only ever saw an ogre sheet twice: once by accident after the camera panned, and once when the ogre became the active unit and the bottom-left card filled in. Took me the whole session and I never got it working reliably. **Fix:** the hitbox.

4. **"37% chance to hit" with no visible reason.** The panel itemised "attack arc FRONT, your nerve −5" and then silently ate 26 points of the target's dodge. I burned two swings before the game popped a tutorial explaining dodge counterplay. The number *is* in the game — Weft's own card reads "SKILL / DODGE 45 / 23" — it's just missing from the line that computes the percentage. **Fix:** add "their dodge −23" to the attack panel and the whole confusion disappears.

5. **Travel committed without showing me the price.** The tutorial said "Click a place to see what the road to it costs, click it again to walk it." My first click on The Wheel-Rut walked it, burning half a day off a three-day payday clock. Later nodes did behave as documented (first click showed "1d"). Roughly one click in three on the map does nothing visible, which makes the two-step ambiguous.

6. **"An Unusual Coin · TRINKET ·" has no effect line.** Blank after the dot. I picked it up in a road event that gave +8 morale; whether wearing it does anything is unknown and the UI doesn't admit it's unknown.

## What I understood, and what I did not

- **Movement — UNDERSTOOD.** 4 hexes, then 3 for a second move in the same turn; the card relabels itself to "3 HEXES · 1/2" after the first. Movement card shows cost in diamonds. UNDO MOVE exists.
- **Actions per turn — UNDERSTOOD.** Two diamonds, cards show ◆ or ◆◆, big skills go on cooldown ("DISENGAGE" showed a "2" after use). 1–9 select, SPACE ends, ESC cancels. All correct as documented.
- **Attacking / hit chance — UNDERSTOOD except one term.** Panel gives "95% CHANCE TO HIT / ARMOUR DAMAGE 11-16 / HITPOINT DAMAGE 11-16 / attack arc BACK +30 · ×1.25 dmg / Reach 1". Itemises arc and both sides' nerve. Does **not** itemise the target's dodge (see above).
- **Armour vs hitpoints — UNDERSTOOD, and I had to derive it.** Two pools; armour reduces hitpoint damage while it lasts. Evidence: same sword on the same man read 11-16 HP damage with 6 armour up and 18-26 with armour at 0. The panel recomputes live, which is the right behaviour; nothing states the rule, but the moving number taught it in two hovers.
- **Facing / arcs — UNDERSTOOD.** BACK +30 ×1.25, FLANK +15 ×1.10, SHOULDER +5, FRONT nothing. Printed on the panel every time.
- **Zone of control / disengage — PARTLY.** The rules page says DISENGAGE "IS the step: one hex". In play it spent an action, granted "CLEAN · +1 HEX" and turned my whole move clean for the turn. Those aren't the same description. I never tested taking a free swing on purpose.
- **Nerve — UNDERSTOOD, and it's the best system here.** Ladder from BROKEN through OK/HAPPY to IT ROCKS. Feeds dodge (55/2 became 55/0 on one rung drop; a broken man's dodge collapsed to 4%). Kills move it three rungs, casting costs the caster 16 and two rungs, hits move it one or two. Flags on the model: 💀 for about-to-die, 🏳 for routing. The "starting rung is a magnet" rule from the rules page I read but never verified.
- **Enemy behaviour — PARTLY.** Hover gives a full "WHAT IT CAN DO TO YOU" list with reach and damage bands, which is excellent. But I could not get it for ogres, and I never saw a preview of what an enemy will do *this* turn — this is not Into the Breach, and it doesn't claim to be.
- **Money — UNDERSTOOD on paper, untested in practice.** ◉195 crowns, ❦8 provisions, ▤0 salvage, ◈0 gems, four stated uses, "wages fall due every four days", payday ◉21 counting down on the top bar. I never got to a payday or a shop, so I never saw the chest actually threatened.
- **Company morale — PARTLY.** A separate bar ("AT EASE", 85→93%) from unit nerve. Moves on road events (+8 from a coin). What it does mechanically, I don't know.
- **Gear — UNDERSTOOD.** Armour/trinket/main/off/bag/pet slots, stash items show deltas against equipped ("▼ STOPS -22", "▼ DAMAGE -4"). Best single idea on that screen.
- **Perks — UNDERSTOOD.** Two per level, one pick, all numeric ("Iron Jaw +12 maximum morale" vs "Duellist +8 to hit while exactly one enemy is adjacent"). Real either/ors.
- **Attributes — NO.** "STRENGTH Strong enough · AGILITY Heavy-handed · INTELLECT Quick to see it · MORALE Steady". Words with no numbers and no hover. I cannot rank two recruits with those, which will matter the moment a muster asks me to choose.
- **Events on the road — PARTLY.** One was a single-button freebie. One was a genuine three-way with costed options. Sample too small.
- **Resting / healing — PARTLY.** "Nothing heals here — only days on the road do that", and "mending 21" sits on a wounded man's roster card. So wounds cost days and/or 21 of something. I never spent it.
- **Food / provisions — NO.** ❦8 never moved in two days of travel.

## The fights
The tavern brawl is a good teacher: one drunk with a labelled "Drunk −12 to hit, −8 dodge" badge, then three, then an ogre, then reinforcements on my side, each step attached to a lesson that was mechanically true. I had a plan every turn except the one where I was surrounded — there I was clicking, because I couldn't read the overlays and the escalation was scripted rather than caused by my mistakes.

Did I know what enemies could do before they did it? For humans, yes, and better than the genre standard: hover gives HP, armour, nerve, movement, skill/dodge, actions, statuses with their numbers, and a "WHAT IT CAN DO TO YOU" list with reach on every attack. For ogres, no, because the hover doesn't work on them — and ogres are the ones I most needed it for, since "PICK UP AND THROW · — · rng 4" is exactly the sort of thing that ruins a plan and its damage column is literally a dash.

Nothing felt unfair once understood. The two things that felt unfair at the time — 45% and 37% swings that missed, and being surrounded three-on-one — turned out to be a dodge stat I could have read and a scripted story beat respectively. The one genuinely undisclosed thing was the ogre throw, and the log explained it clearly the moment it happened ("picks Ratkin, spear up and throws them - 7 hitpoints for the landing. And nowhere near anybody of theirs"), which is close enough to fair.

The best moment in the session was mechanical, not narrative: one spear kill, and every remaining enemy's hit chance on my screen went up at once because their nerve fell and nerve feeds dodge. I could see the death spiral start. That's what I'll remember.

## Reading and looking
The right amount of text in the wrong order. The RULES page is dense but genuinely good and I only wanted to read it once — which is the correct design, since the screen mostly carries it afterward. The in-fight tutorials are well written and mechanically honest, but several arrive one beat too late: the dodge lesson after two wasted swings, "read the word on the hex before shooting" in the second battle, "nothing heals here — only days on the road do that" on log line four.

Prose quality is high throughout and never gets in the way — "On this island, most would rather watch an unfairness than join a fight that already looks lost" is doing worldbuilding and telling me reinforcements are conditional at the same time.

Things I could not find on screen: the target's dodge inside the hit-chance breakdown; the splash footprint of BLIGHT-WIND while aiming a delayed friendly-fire AoE; any effect text for the trinket; any number behind the four attribute adjectives; the damage of PICK UP AND THROW. Nothing was too small to read.

## Three things I would fix first
1. **The oversized-unit hover hitbox.** Ogres are unreadable on the field. This game's whole pitch is that the readout tells you everything, and it fails on the units where it matters most.
2. **Click feedback on the hex grid, and separate the three overlays.** A silent no-op is indistinguishable from a lost click when actions are scarce. Highlight the hex under the cursor; use outline for my reach and fill for their threat.
3. **Put the target's dodge in the hit-chance panel, and paint BLIGHT-WIND's splash while aiming.** Both are numbers the game already knows and shows elsewhere; both currently make me guess in the one moment I'm spending an action.

## Something I liked
The stash comparing itself to what the man is already wearing — "Cook-pot and straps · ARMOUR · stops 12 · ▼ STOPS -22" — in red, at a glance, with no arithmetic. And "Blood on the Road": three options with the fight size, the ally count, the recruits and the loot all printed under the button, interlocking with a standing objective that wanted two more heads. That screen is a better mercenary-company decision than most games in this genre manage in forty hours.
