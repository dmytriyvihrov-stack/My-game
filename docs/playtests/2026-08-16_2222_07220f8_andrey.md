# Andrey - playtest report

## Verdict
It's a grim little medieval scrap on a honeycomb floor, where you nudge a handful of hired men around a tavern and hit people until they either die or lose their nerve and run for the door. The words are lovely — someone here can write. The pictures are lovely too. But the whole machine is printed at about a third of the size my eyes can handle, and half the time when I clicked, nothing happened and nothing told me why. Would I play again: **maybe** — and only because of the writing. The one reason I'd say no is that I spent my last twenty minutes clicking into silence.

## Where I stopped and why
Rule 1: **I would stop.** The concrete moment: my spearwoman Vesna was standing directly beside "the barman", I clicked her spear card, then clicked him, and instead of a thrust I got his statistics card. That was the tenth action in a row that produced no visible result — three failed moves for Vesna, a fireball that silently didn't reach, an arrow that silently didn't reach, a hex-click that did nothing, a MOVE card that did nothing. At that point I put it down. I never saw the tutorial fight end and I never got to "Take the road", which I'd wanted to see.

## Interest over time

| checkpoint | interest | what I was doing | why that score |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Trading blows with the drunk carter, one on one | The teaching cards were plain and good ("Click a green hex to walk, click a man to swing"), but nine tenths of the screen was unreadable smear |
| 2 | 2 | Trying to walk the spearwoman three hexes | Three clicks, zero response, zero explanation. That's when I stopped trusting the game |
| 3 | 2 | Chasing the last two enemies into a far corner with five people | The fight had become an errand. Only the writing kept me on |

High point: the moment the drunk broke and the game said *"His nerve is empty, and the word means RUNNING: a broken man stops swinging and makes for the door."* — followed by *"THREE ON ONE"* and a card showing me the hearth corner and telling me to put my back in it. For about three minutes I felt like I had a plan. Low point: the long silent stretch where nothing I clicked did anything.

## Where I got stuck or confused

1. **Clicking an enemy did not attack him.** I was told "click a man to swing". I clicked the carter, and got a statistics card instead. It took me two more actions to work out that he was two hexes away and the card was a "here's who he is" panel. *What would have saved me:* one line on that panel, or an obvious cursor change.
2. **The stat card sat on top of the man I wanted to hit.** After I moved, my own big card opened over the exact hexes where the enemy was standing. Faintly comic. *Fix:* open it on the free side of the screen.
3. **Moving failed silently, over and over.** Clicked hex → nothing. Clicked the MOVE card then a hex → nothing. No red flash, no sound, no message. I eventually worked out (about six wasted actions later) that the fire pit and the barrels are solid and the game just ignores clicks on them. This happened again with the mage's fire and the archer's bow, presumably out of range. *This is the single biggest problem in the build.* One "no" — a flash, a shake, a line saying "too far" — fixes it.
4. **The game swapped me from one character to another without saying so.** One turn I was "★ You, CAPTAIN", the next the corner said "Vesna Kolb, SPEARWOMAN", with different cards on the bar. I only noticed because the name changed. I never got a moment of "now you have a company; here is everyone."
5. **The enemy's turn is invisible.** My armour went 34 → 31 → 27 → 23 → 11 and I never once saw a blow land or a man swing. Numbers just moved between my clicks. I have no idea who hit me or with what.
6. **He "broke and ran" to the far wall, then next turn was standing next to me hitting me again.** Either he rallied or I misunderstood the rule. Nothing on screen told me which.
7. **I couldn't find a way out.** The "☰ MENU" button only appears sometimes; clicking where it had been did nothing, and Escape did nothing. I wanted to go back and try "Take the road" and I couldn't.

## What I understood, and what I did not

- **Movement** — PARTLY. I know I click a hex and the man walks, roughly four hexes' worth. I never learned which hexes are legal, because blocked ones look much like open ones at this size and refuse in silence.
- **Attacking** — UNDERSTOOD. Click the weapon card, click the man. Big words come up: "MISS", "DODGE", "−20", "BACKSTAB!", "KILLED". That part is clear and satisfying.
- **Actions per turn** — PARTLY. The first card said "Two actions a turn". I later found "ACTIONS 1 of 2" buried in a card. There is no big, obvious counter I could read at a glance.
- **Hit chance / damage preview** — PARTLY. There *are* "95% 21-29" numbers floating over reachable enemies. I only discovered them by leaning right into the screen. At normal size they're speckles.
- **Armour vs health** — UNDERSTOOD, and taught well: *"Iron soaks a blow before flesh does. The grey bar is the harness; the red one under it is the man inside."*
- **Nerve / morale** — UNDERSTOOD in principle, taught very well ("a broken man stops swinging and makes for the door"), but I couldn't track it in practice because the word is tiny. I saw HAPPY, OK, and something green I couldn't read.
- **Facing / flanking** — UNDERSTOOD. "A flank is easier to hit; the small of the back is easier still" and then a "BACKSTAB!" for 31. Lovely bit of cause and effect.
- **Disengage / free swings when you walk away** — UNDERSTOOD as a sentence, never verified.
- **Terrain / corners** — PARTLY. Told clearly ("each lets only one of them reach you at a time"), tried it, and they simply came round both sides of me anyway. Either I picked the wrong corner or the advice doesn't hold.
- **Classes and races** — PARTLY. I met a captain, a spearwoman, a battle-mage and an archer, each with different cards, and an ogre. Flavour understood; numbers not.
- **Enemy behaviour** — NO. I never watched them act.
- **Money, wages, food, gear, resting, events on the road** — NEVER MET. I never got out of the tutorial fight.

## The fights
The one-on-one opening was good. Two men, a fire, clear big words for every result. I felt like I was doing something.

Did I know what the enemies could do before they did it? Actually **yes**, and that's a real strength: clicking one gives "WHAT IT CAN DO TO YOU: CUDGEL 7-13 rch 1, KICK 2-4 rch 1" plus a trait like "Braggart — Lifted by their own kills". I genuinely liked that. The catch is I had to lean into the monitor twice to read one card.

Did I have a plan or was I clicking? A plan for about three minutes (the corner, the backstab). After that, clicking.

Nothing felt *unfair* exactly — I lost armour steadily and it seemed reasonable. What felt unfair was the mopping-up: two survivors ran to the far corner of a big room and I had to walk five people across empty floor to finish them, one END TURN at a time, with an interface that kept ignoring me.

## Reading and looking
Too much text in the small places and exactly the right amount in the big ones. The tutorial cards — big, one paragraph, plain English — were the best part of the build. Everything else was 10-pixel type on a brown wall.

Things the screen said were "too small to read" that actually cost me something:
- **The whole skill bar.** The thing I press most often. I had to squint once just to learn my own weapon was an "ARMING SWORD 16-24" and that there was a "DISENGAGE" and a "SPEAR WALL".
- **My own stats panel, bottom left.** Name, class, armour label, nerve word, actions — all smear. I could only ever read the two big numbers (armour and hitpoints).
- **The hit-chance and damage previews over enemies** ("95% 21-29"). This is the key tactical information in the game and it is the smallest thing on screen.
- **The whole top bar.** I could read "⛊ 5" and "☠ 2" and guessed those were headcounts. Everything between them was unreadable.
- **The enemy inspect card** — good content, unreadable size. Two squints for one card.
- **The line of small text under the three menu buttons on the title screen.** Never found out what it said.
- **The row of portraits down the left edge.** I squinted at them and still couldn't tell friend from foe.

I leaned into the monitor six times in about seventy actions. That's six times too many for a first sitting.

I could never tell at a glance which figures were mine. My people and their people are the same size, the same brown, on the same brown floor. The only reliable marker is a small star, and the star seems to mark *whoever I'm currently moving*, not "my side". Once there were nine bodies on screen I was guessing.

## Three things I would fix first
1. **Never refuse silently.** If a hex is blocked, a target out of range, an action spent — flash it, shake it, say it. Half my session was clicking into a void.
2. **Make the small type big.** Skill bar, stats panel, hit-chance numbers, nerve word. If a thing is a rule the player must use, it cannot be ten pixels tall.
3. **Tell me who is mine.** A coloured ring, a banner, a plain outline round every man on my side — and a clear "you now command five people, here they are" the moment that becomes true.

(A fourth, free one: show me the enemy's turn. Let me see the blow that took my armour off.)

## Something I liked
The writing, unreservedly. "You cannot afford to be good to everyone." "His nerve is empty, and the word means RUNNING: a broken man stops swinging and makes for the door." And best of all, when the bystanders finally got up: *"Strangers have been watching this from the benches. Now they trade one look, and hands find weapons. It took them a while. On this island, most would rather watch an unfairness than join a fight that already looks lost."* I don't play games like this and I still wanted to know what happened next on that island. That's worth something. Also the title screen — the little men walking with spears through the purple wood is a genuinely handsome picture, and "Widowed thrice" as a description of a spearwoman told me more in two words than any of the numbers did.
