## Dima

**Verdict:** GRIMTOLL is a hex-and-two-actions mercenary tactics game with a morale engine at its centre and a road layer with four currencies and a wage clock. The fights are the good part: nerve is not a flavour bar, it feeds dodge

**Stopped:** **Rule 1 — I would stop.** Not from frustration; from having got what I came for. I was in the second fight, three ogres at 112 HP each with the trait "Does not run", i.e. the morale engine I'd just l

**Would fix first:**

1. **The oversized-unit hover hitbox.** Ogres are unreadable on the field. This game's whole pitch is that the readout tells you everything, and it fails on the units where it matters most.
2. **Click feedback on the hex grid, and separate the three overlays.** A silent no-op is indistinguishable from a lost click when actions are scarce. Highlight the hex under the cursor; use outline for my reach and fill for their threat.
3. **Put the target's dodge in the hit-chance panel, and paint BLIGHT-WIND's splash while aiming.** Both are numbers the game already knows and shows elsewhere; both currently make me guess in the one moment I'm spending an action.

**Stopped:** **Stop rule 1: I would stop.** Concrete moment: turn IX of the prologue fight, my captain at 23/57 HP and 0 armour, two broken enemies fleeing around the far side of a tavern. I selected MOVE, clicked

**Would fix first:**

1. **Never let a click do nothing silently.** Out of range, blocked, no line of fire — say so, in one word, where I'm looking. This single thing caused most of my frustration and it's what ended my session.
2. **Show me range and reach on the board.** Light the hexes an attack can reach the moment I select it (and put "REACH 1" / "RANGE 5" on the card face next to the damage, the way MOVE shows "4 HEXES"). Make the walkable-hex tint actually readable over the floor art.
3. **Give me a pause menu on Escape, and make ☰ MENU visible.** I should always be able to leave a fight and go try another mode. Related: let the selected weapon stay selected between swings.
