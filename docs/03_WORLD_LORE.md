# The Grimtoll Lore Book

> **This is the world's canon, and events lean on it.** Rewritten 2026-08-01 on the user's
> instruction: *"Update concept of the world... Light shift in direction of ttw, different races,
> a tiny bit magic. Strange shit happening"*, *"heroes not merceneries, they just start with a
> contract. But rather forced together random crew"*, and the authoring rule that decided the
> whole style of this file: **"It is great to bring specific clear things - not that good
> something vague."**
>
> The old header said this document was deliberately soft. It is not soft any more. The code still
> depends on none of it (names live in content, not in logic), so it CAN be rewritten, but the
> events now quote it, so rewrite it knowingly: **when an event and this book disagree, one of
> them is a bug. Decide which.**
>
> The one style law: **every strange thing here has one clear, observable rule and no explanation
> behind the rule.** A player can learn the rule. Nobody, in the world or out of it, gets the
> explanation. That is the whole method: specific about WHAT, silent about WHY.

## 1. The world in one paragraph

**One enormous island, far enough from everywhere that leaving is a rumour.** Three peoples live
on it: the **ratkin**, who were always here, the **ogres**, who were always here first (both
peoples say this), and the **humans**, who arrived sixty years ago on ships and have spent sixty
years finding out what the island thinks of confidence. Magic is real, rare, and moves like
weather. Strange things happen, and they are not everywhere: they are specific, they keep rules,
and sensible people hang a bell where one was seen and walk the long way round. The island is
called **Grimtoll**, and the name is not decoration: everything here charges for passage.

---

## 2. The name: tolls and bells

**Grimtoll is named twice in one word.**

**The toll:** every road on this island is owned. A clan cuts its mark on a milestone, a steading
elder sits across a road and names a price, a dead kingdom's collector still stamps a ledger, a
bridge has an owner again by morning. Passage is never free; when it looks free, the price has
simply not been named yet. *(This is the pillar written into geography: you cannot afford to be
good to everyone, because everyone is charging.)*

**The tolling:** the island keeps watch with **bells**. The custom is older than the humans and
all three peoples keep it:

- **You hang a bell where something wrong has been seen.** A bell is a warning that goes on
  working while you sleep. New wire on a gate-post means it happened recently.
- **Three bells over a door** is a boast and an advertisement: *nothing wrong has crossed this
  threshold in three generations.* The tavern at Grausen Hold is called the Sign of the Three
  Bells, and earns it, mostly.
- **Stringing a road with bells to HUNT** is the custom turned inside out, and even clans that
  fight each other agree it is obscene. One clan in the east does it anyway. See §9.
- A bell that rings flat is not replaced. It is left to say so.

## 3. The three peoples

**Races are an ordinary fact here.** Nobody on Grimtoll is surprised by a ratkin or an ogre;
mixed camps, mixed markets and mixed grudges are just what a crossroads looks like. The only
people still capable of surprise are fresh humans, and the Captain's crew is exactly that: the
prologue keeps them ignorant on purpose, and BLOOD ON THE ROAD is where the island corrects them.

### The ratkin: clever, numerous, and shoddy

- **Technologically the most advanced people on the island**: mechanisms, metallurgy, drainage,
  siege work, waterworks.
- **And they manufacture rubbish, brilliantly.** A ratkin crossbow is a marvel of engineering
  that fires eleven times and comes apart. This is a strategy, not a failure: they can make ten
  thousand of anything in a season, and it does not matter that each one is bad.
- Organised into **clans**. Their history is mostly glorious, extensively sung wars of ratkin
  against ratkin. Ask about the island's six great battles and a ratkin will name six, all of
  them clan against clan.
- **A clan that owns a road cuts true miles on it.** Honest milestones are law, pride and
  advertisement in one object; falsifying one is murder done by chisel, and clans hang people for
  it.
- Family is aggressive, contradictory and politically useful. An aunt can be claimed the way
  ground can be claimed, and settled the same way: in front of a third party.
- **Not an enemy race.** There are clans you trade with and clans that will hang you from your
  own bells. The rule "no race is an enemy race" (concept §4) is load-bearing.

### The ogres: enormous, slow, and left alone

- **Big, strong, and not stupid so much as uninterested.** Ogres think slowly and do not consider
  it a flaw.
- They live where nobody else wants to: high cold ground on and around **the Hunch**, deep wet
  ground elsewhere. This is why ogres and ratkin have shared an island for ages: they rarely want
  the same square mile.
- Their steadings farm badly and hunt well. What an ogre wants, in almost every case, is **to not
  be disturbed**. An ogre in a travelling crew is there for a reason (debt, exile, curiosity, a
  grudge) and the reason is always worth asking about.
- An ogre bargain is short and kept. "Road's ours today. Was yours yesterday. Be yours again
  tomorrow" is a complete legal system.

### The humans: late, arrogant, and now useful

- Arrived **sixty years ago** (two generations) on the ships: better armour, some book-magic,
  total confidence. The first years went well. Then it turned out that being confident is not the
  same as being formidable, and the island noticed.
- **Militarily they are a joke, and the joke is earned.** Human companies broke early and often
  in the first wars, and ogres in particular find the material inexhaustible.
- **What humans are genuinely better at is growing things**: crop rotation, drainage, breeding,
  preservation. A human agronomist can double a clan's yield. That is why humans are tolerated
  and employed, and why nobody fears them, and both of those facts are why they are still alive.
- Human holdings are now scattered and cannot bring force at scale. **Grausen Hold** in the west
  is the strongest thing they still have. **Ashmoor** in the east was the last human works of any
  size, and it fell last winter (§9).

## 4. Sixty years of humans: the timeline

Events should hang numbers on this spine and not invent new ones.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **60 years ago** | **The Landing.** The ships arrive. Chapels, holds and foundries go up. One wreck of that fleet still stands sixty years up a shingle beach, too far from any water that could have floated it. |
| **~58 to 40 years ago** | **The Shorecrown**: the landing lords crown a human kingdom. It lasts one generation. Its wars against the clans are where human military reputation died. |
| **40 years ago** | **The Shorecrown falls.** No successor. Its writs, tolls and uniforms survive it in odd places; one collector still works its roads in complete seriousness. |
| **~30 years ago** | **The Bloom begins rising out of the Black Fen** (§6). The peat-cutters notice first. The ratkin quietly re-man their waterworks. |
| **3 years ago** | **The last ship.** One late lord buys a sixty-year-old map, raises a retinue at home, and lands. The Captain is in that retinue. |
| **Last winter** | **Ashmoor falls.** The winter takes the foundry-master; a clan takes the foundry. Its garrison scatters; some of the scattered are still walking the roads with half a uniform each. |
| **The thaw, this spring** | **Skelbrook falls.** The clan takes the hall of a lesser man of Grausen Hold's, at the far end of the valley, and strings the roads behind it. He starts writing to his lord every week. |
| **This spring** | The lord has read those letters for a season and done nothing. A coin lands on a table at the Three Bells. **The game starts here.** |

## 5. Magic: a weather system, not a toolkit

- **Magic is a limited natural resource that moves in currents.** Those who can read the currents
  are called wizards, and what they mostly read is that today there is not very much.
- **Learning it takes years and pays badly**, so almost every wizard also keeps a trade: a sword,
  book-keeping, medicine. A caster who can do nothing else is a caster who starves in a dry
  season.
- **Mechanically (built): casting costs the caster's own mood.** The limit on magic is the
  person doing it. A battle-mage swings a short sword half his rounds because the working takes
  more out of him than the sword does. The big workings are slow and can be stopped; the small
  ones are small. That is the whole of "a tiny bit of magic": real, priced, never the answer to
  everything.
- **Ratkin do not cast so much as smell.** A clan keeps a **warp-sniffer**: a specialist who
  reads currents the way a dowser reads water, and does the clan's one working when the current
  allows. Nobody stands next to the sniffer, including the clan.
- **Humans brought book-magic off the ships**, and it is the one human import the clans took
  seriously. Most of the books did not survive the Shorecrown. The people who memorised them did.

## 6. The strange: specific things with rules

*"Strange shit happening"* is a pillar of the world, and it obeys the style law: **one observable
rule per thing, no explanation behind it.** These are canon. Events may reference them by name.

### The Bloom

A current of magic that **stopped moving and started soaking**, the way a river stops in a marsh.
It has been rising out of the **Black Fen** for about thirty years and is wider every year the
peat-cutters look.

Its rules, all observable:
- **Light with no source, the wrong colour.** Moss goes black under it. Nothing dies and nothing
  is well.
- **It gets in through openings**: a cut, a long submersion, an afternoon of breathing fen-water.
  What comes out afterwards is changed (the mutation chains: the gills, the nail). It does not
  get in through unbroken skin, which is why the sensible walk through it with their sleeves
  down and their mouths shut.
- **Water that moves does not bloom.** This is why the ratkin have kept their old fen-edge
  waterworks running for thirty years at a loss, and why standing pools are the thing to watch.
- **It creeps.** Camp at its edge and by morning the edge is closer.

### The Fen-Mother

A fen-beast. Her kind were always in the deep fen and were never this size: **the Bloom has been
feeding her for thirty years**, and the bloom-light runs under her hide in slow bands. The small
one behind her is **the first of her kind born under the Bloom**, and she holds ground over it
the way anything holds ground over a child. She is not a monster with a lair; she is a mother
with a territory, and every choice on her card knows it.

### The Thing in Armour

A harness of **landing-pattern human plate**, sixty years old, the kind nobody on this island has
made since the ships. It walks a dead order along the road it was set to hold.

Its rules: **it keeps to the road's middle. It does not hurry, does not parley, and does not
stop.** Broken ground does not shake it; it is the same distance behind you at dusk as it was at
noon. Farms it passes hang a bell.

### The Old Work

Dressed stone older than any clan's songs, found here and there across Grimtoll, **always still
doing its job**: the warm spring's cut kerbing, the door that stands in a field with no house,
the sitting stones that are always exactly the right height. The ogres say it was always here,
and on this island that passes for a guarantee. Nobody living made it; all three peoples leave it
alone or leave offerings, because **a shrine here is not where a god is. It is where something
has been seen to work.**

### Saints and their bones

The human religion came off the ships: saints, litanies, chapels (one of them now stands in four
inches of brown water). Most of what survives is **the words**, which some people still know, and
**the bones**: a real saint's fingerbone stays **faintly warm**, and that is how you know a real
one. Nobody has an explanation. The bones do not care.

### Rat-kings

The clans do not discuss rat-kings with outsiders, or much with each other. What is canon: a
**knuckle-bone with a crown scratched into it** is a thing a clan would pay to have not exist,
and a thing that does not like being kept. It moves when unwatched. It is always found again in
easy reach.

### The red warg

*(Added 2026-08-02, because a trinket needed it: the Red-Warg Bottle in
[backlog #78](00_PLAN_AND_BACKLOG.md). The item is specced and not built. The animal is canon from
here whether or not the item ever ships.)*

**An animal, and the island is firm about that.** Nobody hangs a bell for a warg, and the reason
given is always the same: a bell is for a strange thing, and a warg is a big dog on the high ground.
Everyone says this. Everyone also knows what it does.

Its rules, all observable:

- **It does not tire when it should, and then it stops.** A warg runs a horse into the ground and
  then holds the same stride for as long again, uphill, in snow, carrying whatever it has already
  killed. And then it lies down. Wherever it is. For about a day nothing will make it get up: not
  noise, not fire, not being stood over. **Hunters do not chase a warg. They follow it and wait.**
  This is not thought remarkable. It is thought convenient.
- **Its blood stays the colour it came out.** Everything else browns inside the hour. Warg blood is
  still bright a month on, in a bottle, in the cold, in the dark, and that is the whole of the
  trade: a pedlar can prove what he is selling by holding it up, and cannot fake it by holding
  something else up. The red in the name is the blood. The animal is grey.
- **A mouthful buys one hard effort you did not have, and then you take the animal's hour.** You get
  up when you could not have got up, you do the thing, and afterwards you sit down where you are
  standing and are no use to anybody for a while. Everyone who has drunk it describes the second
  half the same way and drinks it again anyway.
- **They come off the Hunch, and only in bad winters.** They are dry-ground animals; the Fen has
  nothing to do with them and neither does the Bloom, which is worth saying plainly because
  everything strange on this island gets blamed on the Fen eventually and this one predates the
  complaint.

**Nobody has an explanation and nobody is looking hard.** They have been cut open. There is nothing
in one that is not in a dog.

**The ogres, who live where wargs live, decline to be impressed.** Their position, delivered slowly
and more than once, is that anything which has to run like that has planned its day badly.

### The road's own people

Some things met on roads are not explained by any of the above and stay that way: a fiddler with
no shoes whose tune turns out to be a list of place-names in order, ending where you are going; a
woman who sweeps the ground in front of a door and knows which way the bells are. They keep the
style law too: what they DO is perfectly clear.

## 7. The company: not mercenaries

> **The user's ruling, 2026-08-01, verbatim:** *"heroes not merceneries, they just start with a
> contract. But rather forced together random crew."*

**You are not a mercenary company. You are what was in the room.** Four strangers with empty
purses in a tavern when a coin hit a table: a ruined captain, a woman who was already standing, a
man who arrived late, a woman who counted the guards first. Nothing about the crew is a
profession. They did not choose each other; most of them would not have.

**They start with one contract**, because a coin landed in front of them, and the contract is the
only thing holding them together on day one. What holds them together on day twenty (if anything
does) is what the player spent: that is the game.

**The island will call them mercenaries anyway**: sell-swords, scum, a company. The words do the
island's work and the game keeps them where the island speaks. Wages, contracts and the Captain's
authority all stay exactly as built (a crew still eats, and the purse is still the clock). The
layering rule from the outside review holds: **layer the language, never delete the premise.**

**What the word "company" is allowed to mean**: on the run report and in the world's mouth, fine.
In the game's own narration, prefer **the crew, the company (small c), you** and never "the
mercenaries" as self-description.

## 8. The Captain's ruin

> This is the opening premise, and the piece most worth keeping stable: the Captain's identity
> hangs off it. **The user's ask for the onboarding, 2026-08-01: the Captain "head ambision
> before, but things haven't been worked propery for him."**

You are **human**. Three years ago you came in on **the last ship**, in the retinue of a lord who
had bought a sixty-year-old map and believed it. You believed it too: you were somebody, with
skills, connections, a decent harness, prospects. **You had ambitions, and you can name them**,
which is worse than having had none.

The lord lasted two years, learned nothing, and started a war with established clans. It took one
season. Your people were destroyed; you got out with the harness you stand in, a head full of
contacts, and a purse that is now empty. The last year has been the slow finding-out of what a
human with good armour and no lord is worth on this island. The tavern is where that year ends,
one way or the other.

**Two threads deliberately open for later:**
1. **People from your old life turn up**: human, ratkin and ogre alike. Some owe you. Some do not
   want to see you.
2. **Your former lord.** Whether he actually died is established nowhere, on purpose.

## 9. The contract and the east road

**The employer:** the man in the very good coat at the Three Bells is **the lord of Grausen
Hold**, the strongest human holding left. He is not a villain; he is a human with money, and the
Captain does not like most of his own kind either.

**The contract, rewritten 2026-08-02 on the user's ask and now canon.** It used to be a rumour
("something east is eating my road, and my factor has stopped writing"). It is a **place, a man
and a date**:

- **Skelbrook.** A small holding at the **far end of the valley**, past the Hunch: **one village
  and one hall**, held for the lord by a lesser man of his. **The clan took the hall at the
  thaw** and has been sitting in it since.
- **The letter.** That man writes to Grausen Hold **every week**. The lord has been reading those
  letters since the thaw and doing nothing about them, because his own men are worth keeping and
  Skelbrook is not. **A company at forty crowns is the cheapest thing that counts as doing
  something.** He never says this; the prologue narrates it once and leaves the arithmetic to the
  player.
- **What is actually bought:** **bring back the head out of that chair**, keep the purse. **A head,
  and not a village.** That is why the act can honestly end on the road with Skelbrook still taken,
  and the Snare victory card says so out loud.
  ⚑ **#156 CUT "carry the letter east" AS A JOB** *(user: "So deliver letter or kill - i prefere
  kill")*. It was a second objective the player is never given a way to discharge, standing in
  front of the one the whole act ends on. **The letter above stays exactly as it is**, because it
  is not the errand, it is the man: he has been reading them since the thaw and doing nothing, and
  the prologue still says so in his own mouth. What went is the task, not the fact.
- ⚠ **HE SAYS "BANDITS".** They are the Snare Clan. A lord who has not been east in years calling
  a clan "bandits" is the exact shape of the prologue's set-up: the player is meant to believe
  this is a country with one people in it until **Blood on the Road**. Nothing in the tap-room
  may correct him.
- **He has bought a company for this before.** Nine of them are in a ditch two miles short of the
  bells, and he did not mention them at the Three Bells. That silence is canon, and the Dead
  Company card is where the crew finds out what it means.
- **Skelbrook itself is next act's problem.** It is not on the built map and no node reaches it.

**The geography of the run (matches the built map):** the whole act is fought in **the Grausen
valley** - hills along its north edge, **the Hunch** closing the east, the fen belt and the water
down the south-west. *(The name is on the map since 2026-08-02, faint, in the middle band. The
word was already in the world's mouth: "a clan war two valleys south".)* Grausen Hold and the
Three Bells in the west → the fork roads → the Ruined Steading → the Muster Field → **the Black
Fen** in the south-west, where the Bloom rises and the Fen-Mother holds her ground →
**Coldharrow**, forty houses and a civil wall, the act's one human village → the Stone Field or
the quiet road → the Dead Company, where the Thing in Armour finds you → the Warm Spring → the
shrine → the Last Muster → **the Snare**, at the southern foot of **the Hunch**. Skelbrook lies
two days beyond it, off the map.

**The Snare Clan.** The clan on the Snare's standard calls itself something in clan-speech that
outsiders are not offered; the road calls them **the Snare Clan**, and the road named them. Two
facts bind them:
1. **They took Ashmoor's foundry last winter** and could not work all of it (two furnaces have
   never been lit since).
2. **They string roads with trap-bells and take travellers**, in hauling-cages built for boar.
   Bells are for warning on this island; every other clan finds the trap-bells obscene, which the
   Snare Clan considers a compliment.
3. **And they took Skelbrook's hall at the thaw** (§9). Their standard has flown over it since,
   which is what makes the head at the Snare the head the lord asked for. The victory card says
   this in one sentence and no other card in the act mentions Skelbrook at all.

The woman in the cage at the Snare is **Wynn Aldreth**, the Ashmoor foundry-master's widow: the
clan that took her town is the clan that took her, and she has had three days in the cage to
appreciate the symmetry. The head the lord wants is under the Snare's standard. Whether taking it
fixes anything is next act's problem.

people needs no meter.

## 11. The rule that keeps the world honest

**There are no bad guys, only interests.** Ogres want to be left alone. Clans want what clans
want, mostly from each other. Humans want to still be here in fifty years. The Bloom wants
nothing anybody can name, which is why it frightens people who are frightened of nothing else.

And the crew is not the hero of anything: on most days it is the least sympathetic party present,
a crowd of armed strangers walking somebody else's road for somebody else's coin. Every enemy
gets one line that makes killing them a choice. Your own people are the conscience, and they are
watching you.

## 12. Still deliberately open

Enough is decided to write events. These stay open until an event actually needs them, and no
event should quietly close one:

- What made the Old Work, and what is on the other side of the door.
- Whether the lord who brought the Captain is alive.
- Whether ships could ever come again.
- What the Fen-Mother's kind were before the fen, and how many more there are.
- The island's own religions: what ogres and clans pray to, if anything. (Human saints are
  settled in §6.)
- The name of the sea, and what the ratkin and ogre names for the island are.
