# Steam store copy: the reference shelf, measured

**Measured 2026-08-21** against the live Steam store, in English, US pricing.
Fifteen pages: the nine commercial comparables from
[`PRICING_RESEARCH.md`](PRICING_RESEARCH.md) plus the six design references named in
[`docs/01_GAME_CONCEPT.md`](../docs/01_GAME_CONCEPT.md) (Wartales, Darkest Dungeon, The Banner
Saga, Wildermyth, Slay the Spire, Caves of Qud).

**How it was taken.** `store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails` for the copy, prices, genres and
release dates; `store.steampowered.com/appreviews` for the review totals; the store page HTML for
the user tags. Both scripts are in [`steam_refs/`](steam_refs/) and re-run in about a minute, so
every number below can be refreshed rather than trusted.

⚠ **This file holds the SHAPE of the pages, not the pages.** Store copy is somebody else's
copyrighted marketing text, so what is recorded here is structure, word counts, section order and
short evidence fragments. The links go to the real thing, and the raw responses were cached locally
while this was written rather than committed. **If you want to read a page whole, open the link.**

## 3. The patterns

### 1. The short description is 40 words and does three jobs in three sentences

Every one of the fifteen: **name the genre, name your role, name the verbs.** The range is 32 to 47
words, and Steam's 300-character cap is what sets it.

Two openings, and both put the genre word inside the first sentence:

- **imperative, 8 of 15**: Lead / Defend / Command / Become / Manage / Guide / Inhabit / Live.
  Battle Brothers opens *"Lead a mercenary company in this tactical RPG"*.
- **declarative, 7 of 15**: TITLE is a GENRE. Urtuk, Shogun Showdown, Path of Achra, The Dungeon
  Beneath, Wartales, Darkest Dungeon, Wildermyth.

**13 of 15 name a genre word in the FIRST sentence.** The two that do not (The Last Spell, The
Banner Saga) name it in the last clause of the last sentence instead. Nobody leaves it out.

**The verbs are the middle sentence and they are the game loop, listed.** Recruit, train, lead;
explore, recruit, collect bounties, unravel; customize, build, discover. Three or four verbs, comma
separated, no adjectives on them.

**3 of 15 close on a hook rather than a fact**: a question (Darkest Dungeon on keeping heroes
together, Our Adventurer Guild on whether the guild survives you) or the title used as a battle cry
(Slay the Spire).

### 2. Nobody spends more than about sixty words on lore before naming the genre

The body opens one of two ways:

- **World first, then the genre sentence, in the SAME paragraph.** Battle Brothers spends two
  sentences on the fractured world and then says outright that it is a turn-based tactical RPG where
  you lead a mercenary company. Wartales takes 42 words of empire-and-plague before doing the same.
  The Last Spell and Our Adventurer Guild take 35 to 43.
- **The genre sentence flat, as line one.** Urtuk, Darkest Dungeon and The Dungeon Beneath reuse
  their short description verbatim as the first line of the body.

**Zero of fifteen open with a page of fiction.** The longest lore run-up in the set is Wartales at
42 words, and even that lands the genre in the next paragraph.

### 3. The modern length for this shelf is 150 to 300 words, and the long pages are the old expensive ones

Sorted by body length, the four shortest are all 2024 releases at $9.99 to $14.99 (Shogun Showdown
74, Path of Achra 177, Mortal Glory 219, Our Adventurer Guild 292). The four longest are 2014 to
2021 at $24.99 to $34.99, plus Caves of Qud, a fifteen-year project whose page is partly a history.

**Battle Brothers at 1,440 words is the outlier of the set, not the model to copy.** It is a
$29.99 sandbox from 2017 selling breadth, and its page reads like a manual on purpose.

Shogun Showdown is the other end: **74 words, five bullets, nothing else**, and 6,994 reviews at
95%. A short page is not a weak page.

### 4. Sections are four to eight, and a section is a label plus twenty to sixty words

The Iron Oath is the cleanest example in the set: 302 words, 8 headings, and every heading carries
exactly one paragraph of 23 to 37 words. The Dungeon Beneath does the same at 148 words with four.
Wildermyth runs eight labelled sections at 40 to 70 words each.

**Only Battle Brothers and Caves of Qud stack three or more paragraphs under one idea.**

### 5. Two labelling systems, and the choice says what kind of game it is

- **Noun labels**: Features · Characters · Story · Core Features · Key Features. Urtuk, Slay the
  Spire, Banner Saga, Our Adventurer Guild.
- **Verb and benefit phrases**: The Iron Oath's *"Lead wisely, die foolishly"*, Wildermyth's
  *"Choices That Matter"* and *"A New Approach to Death"*, Darkest Dungeon's *"Reclaim Your
  Ancestor's Estate"*.

The story-forward games use the second system, and it is the one that matches RabbleBound's own
voice rules: a heading that is a decision reads like the game, a heading that says Features reads
like a spec sheet.

### 6. Art carries the structure, and the top of the page is a looping clip of the actual fight

Battle Brothers, Mortal Glory 2, Wartales and Caves of Qud put **3 to 13 inline images and muted
autoplay webms** into the description and use them where a heading would be. Battle Brothers' very
first element, above one word of copy, is a looping clip of combat.

**Of fifteen pages, only two carry no inline media at all** (The Banner Saga, from 2014, and
Wildermyth, which uses five stills and no clips).

⚑ **This is the most actionable finding for us.** The page is a ladder of picture, caption, picture,
caption. RabbleBound already has painted 1672x941 event stages and a hex board that animates; a
looping clip of one hex exchange plus one road card is a store asset the build can produce today.

### 7. Countable content is the proof, and every page that sells depth states numbers

Slay the Spire: 350+ cards, 200+ items, 50+ encounters, 50+ events, four characters. Caves of Qud:
70 mutations, 24 castes, four play modes. Darkest Dungeon: 16 hero classes. Banner Saga: 25
playable characters, two races. Path of Achra: 50+ powers.

**No page in the set asks you to take its depth on trust.** A vague claim of variety without a
figure appears nowhere.

### 8. Second person, present tense, and your job title inside the first ten words

Mercenary company leader, Guild Master, the one who reclaims the estate. The you/your density runs
2.6 to 9.5 per hundred words with a median near 4.7, and the low end is the two pages with the most
third-person fiction in them (Darkest Dungeon, Urtuk).

### 9. Failure is sold, never softened

Darkest Dungeon leads on stress and affliction. The Last Spell states plainly that you will fail a
lot. Urtuk promises a harsh and unforgiving realm. Shogun Showdown turns death into the start of the
journey. Perma Death is a tag on five of the fifteen and Difficult on five.

**Nobody apologises for their difficulty in the copy, and nobody promises fairness.**

### 10. Naming your references is normal

Path of Achra names Tales of Maj'Eyal and Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup in the body. Slay the Spire
states its formula as card games fused with roguelikes. Wildermyth compares itself to tabletop
roleplaying in its first sentence.

### 11. The last block is short, and it is an invitation

Path of Achra closes on the Obelisk and the rising flood; Darkest Dungeon on descending at your
peril; Wildermyth on where your myth leads; Urtuk on a two-word send-off. **The closer is one line,
never a summary paragraph.**

### 12. Awards, roadmaps and Early Access notes live at the bottom, with one instructive exception

Darkest Dungeon carries ten award bullets, last. Banner Saga's trilogy note, last. Caves of Qud's
nine-years-of-development paragraph, last. The Iron Oath's Discord banner is the only thing above
About the Game, and Wartales is the exception that proves the rule: **its 2026 roadmap sits at the
very top**, because it is a live-service-shaped product still shipping content, and the roadmap is
the pitch.

## 4. Page skeletons, in order

Read these as architecture rather than as copy.

**Battle Brothers** (1,440 w) - looping combat clip · world hook 2 sentences + genre sentence +
the four decisions you make · the world, travel, contracts, trade, crises · combat, equipment,
action points, morale, injuries · the men, backgrounds, traits, no classes · a closing restatement
of freedom of choice. No text headings anywhere.

**Wartales** (375 w) - 2026 roadmap · About the Game · 3 story paragraphs · 6 feature paragraphs
under banner images (lead a group, customize, journey, contracts, tactical combat, camp life).

**The Last Spell** (538 w) - 2 story paragraphs · what the game is · the day/night loop · then five
labelled bullet groups: the RPG package, the hordes, the Haven, the roguelite elements, the
soundtrack. The soundtrack gets its own section with the composer named.

**Urtuk** (460 w) - genre sentence, verbatim from the blurb · Features, 5 bullets · Dynamic battles
· Characters · **Story, last**. The 205 words of lore are at the bottom of the page, not the top.

**The Iron Oath** (302 w) - Discord · About the Game · pitch paragraph · five headed sections:
high-stakes combat, a gritty saga, an ever-evolving overworld, control your company's destiny, forge
your team, lead wisely die foolishly.

**Our Adventurer Guild** (292 w) - a two-sentence story hook that hands you the job (your friend
died and named you successor) · what the game is · Features with mini-labels: guild master,
management, turn-based battles, memorable characters, build your adventurer, crafting, the cast.

**Shogun Showdown** (74 w) - five bullets. Combat, tiles, deck, roguelike, setting and art style.
Nothing else.

**Path of Achra** (177 w) - a demo note · one poetic line · the roguelikes it descends from · build
customization · the synergy pitch · a caution about brutal enemies · the closing hook.

**Mortal Glory 2** (219 w) - six paragraphs, each under its own image: a concrete battlefield moment
first (a barrel rolled into an enemy), then races, relics and perks, difficulty settings, events,
replayability.

**The Dungeon Beneath** (148 w) - genre sentence verbatim from the blurb · four headed sections of
one paragraph each: craft your party, an ever-changing dungeon, fast-paced battles, endless variety.

**Darkest Dungeon** (302 w) - language note · About the Game · one-sentence genre statement ·
Reclaim Your Ancestor's Estate, 52 words · Core Features, 8 bullets led by the Affliction System ·
a question and a two-word closer · Awards and Honors, 10 bullets.

**The Banner Saga** (310 w) - one 74-word narrative paragraph · Key Features, 6 bullets of 28 to 41
words each, opening with player choice and closing with the multiplayer mode · a note that this is
part one of a trilogy.

**Wildermyth** (637 w) - a title line about hearing a tale or spinning your own · About the Game ·
three paragraphs · three bullets on heroes, history and legacy · then eight labelled sections:
papercraft world, character depth, choices, death, combat, co-op, replayability, the pantheon.

**Slay the Spire** (214 w) - the developers' own first-person sentence about fusing card games and
roguelikes · Features, 3 long bullets · a section that says the game left Early Access, with 7
countable-content bullets under it.

**Caves of Qud** (674 w) - award-winning genre sentence · a do-anything line · seven ALL-CAPS
lead-ins (deep physical simulation, fully simulated creatures, factions, setting, tactical gameplay,
RPG elements, soundtrack) · the character builder with its counts · four play modes · what the 1.0
release added · a closing paragraph on nine years of development.

## 5. What this implies for the RabbleBound page

Not decisions, just what the shelf says. The page itself is a separate job.

1. **Target 250 to 350 words of body and about 40 for the blurb.** That is the median of the shelf
   and the norm for a $14.99 2024-plus release. The itch text in
   [`docs/ITCH_PAGE.md`](../docs/ITCH_PAGE.md) is already close in shape and is the draft to cut
   from.
2. **The genre sentence has to land in the first paragraph.** Our current itch opener leads with
   the pitch line about not affording to be good to everyone, then gives the fiction, then names the
   genre in paragraph three. On the shelf that is one paragraph too late.
3. **Name the job in the first ten words.** Captain of a company nobody chose is the strongest
   asset the copy has and it is exactly the shape the shelf uses.
4. **Five or six verb-phrase headings, one paragraph each.** The Iron Oath's architecture is the
   closest match to what RabbleBound is and it costs 302 words.
5. **State countable content.** Numbers of authored road events, hand-written cards, enemy kinds,
   named backgrounds, hours per run. Every page in the set does this and we have the counts already
   in the build.
6. **The failure economy is the pitch, not a warning.** Wages every fourth day, permanent injuries,
   people who remember what you spent them on. The shelf sells exactly this and never softens it.
7. **Budget the art before the words.** Two or three looping clips (a hex exchange, a road card, the
   world map) plus banner headers are what the top pages are built out of, and the build can already
   render all three.
8. **Hex Grid is a differentiator and not a discovery tag.** One page in fifteen carries it. Put the
   hex in the copy, and spend the top tag slots on Turn-Based Tactics, Tactical RPG, Party-Based RPG,
   Dark Fantasy, Story Rich and Choices Matter, which is where this shelf actually lives.

## 6. Open questions for the next pass

- **Do we sell the roguelike run or the company?** The shelf splits: Battle Brothers, Wartales and
  Iron Oath sell a company you keep; Shogun, Achra and The Last Spell sell a run you repeat.
  RabbleBound is currently written as both and the blurb can only carry one.
- **Does the demo get named in the blurb?** Path of Achra spends its first body block on its demo.
  We have a free browser build already live, which is a stronger version of the same move.
- **Where does the tabletop half go?** Wildermyth's first sentence names tabletop roleplaying and it
  is the closest thing on the shelf to what our event cards do. Nobody else in the set claims it.
