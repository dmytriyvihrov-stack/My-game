# RabbleBound — pricing research

**Market snapshot:** 1 August 2026  
**Product considered:** premium PC tactical roguelike / tactical RPG for Steam  
**Current recommendation:** **$14.99 base price with a 10% launch discount ($13.49)**

## Executive recommendation

For the planned three-act, approximately 10-hour RabbleBound release, use **$14.99** as the working Steam base price.

- Launch at **10% off**, or $13.49 in the US.
- Use Steam's regional-pricing tools rather than manually applying one exchange rate worldwide.
- Move into 15–20% discounts after meaningful updates or appropriate Steam sales.
- Do not rush into 40–50% discounts: this weakens the perceived value of the game and trains wishlisters to wait.

Alternative scope-based prices:

| Release scope | Recommended price |
|---|---:|
| Act 1 only, sold in Early Access | $11.99–12.99 |
| Three acts, approximately 10 hours | **$14.99** |
| 10–15 hours, highly polished presentation, strong demo reception | $17.99 |
| $19.99 or more | Not recommended for the current first-release plan |

A free Act 1 demo followed by a complete $14.99 release is likely a stronger positioning choice than selling a content-light Act 1 as version 1.0.

## How the sales estimates were produced

The copy figures above are **not reported sales**. They use a deliberately broad review-to-sales, or Boxleiter, range of approximately **20–50 Steam purchases per purchaser review**.

The actual multiplier varies with release year, price, genre, audience behaviour, bundles and key sales. The estimates should therefore be used only to distinguish orders of magnitude—not to forecast exact revenue.

Methodology references:

## What price does to unit sales

There is no reliable universal rule such as “reduce price by 20% and sell 40% more.” Public market data is observational: cheaper games are often shorter, simpler, more viral or aimed at a different audience.

The break-even arithmetic is nevertheless exact:

| Price change from $14.99 | Unit-sales change required to preserve gross revenue |
|---|---:|
| Reduce to $9.99 | Must sell **50% more copies** |
| Reduce to $12.99 | Must sell **15.4% more copies** |
| Raise to $17.99 | Can lose up to **16.7% of copies** |
| Raise to $19.99 | Can lose up to **25% of copies** |

This is why $9.99 is not automatically the safe indie choice. It requires half again as many buyers as $14.99 merely to produce the same gross revenue.

The comparable set also shows that price is not the dominant explanation of outcomes:

- Path of Achra succeeded at $9.99, but Mortal Glory 2 sold far fewer copies at $11.99.
- Several $14.99 games range from hundreds to thousands of reviews.
- The Last Spell supports $24.99 with excellent reception and a strong presentation; The Iron Oath at the same price has substantially fewer and less positive reviews.

For an unknown indie, discoverability and perceived quality usually have greater leverage than moving the price by two or three dollars. The important variables are:

- strength of the capsule and one-sentence concept;
- quality and completion of the demo;
- pre-launch wishlists;
- early review score and review velocity;
- streamer and genre-community coverage;
- whether the release feels complete rather than prototype-like.

## Broader Steam price context

GameDiscoverCo examined the first month of sales for the top 50 new non-free Steam games by copies sold. Its median price fell from $19.50 in February 2023 to approximately **$15.64 in October 2025**. When ranked by launch revenue instead of copies, the October 2025 median was higher at approximately **$20.35**.

This suggests a practical split:

- prices at or below $20 are increasingly common among unit-selling hits;
- higher prices can still maximize revenue, but require stronger perceived value, an established IP or a more obviously premium production.

## Why $14.99 fits RabbleBound

At the planned scope, RabbleBound offers more than a small systems-only roguelike:

- a three-act campaign of approximately 10 hours;
- repeatable runs and world memory;
- party management for 4–8 characters;
- tactical hex combat with facing, morale, injuries and character loss;
- illustrated narrative events and branching node maps;
- multiple races, classes and party compositions.

At the same time, this is a first commercial release without an established IP. The current presentation and AI-assisted art pipeline make a $19.99+ comparison against polished studio-scale tactical RPGs harder to win. A $14.99 price communicates a substantial specialist indie without demanding The Last Spell-level production value.

## Discount plan

Recommended starting cadence:

1. Launch: 10% discount for 7–14 days.
2. First meaningful update or suitable sale: 15–20%.
3. Later lifecycle: gradually move through 25%, 33% and deeper discounts only when the sales curve and content plan justify it.

Steam states that launch discounts must run for 7–14 days and recommends increasing discount depth gradually. It warns that very high launch discounts or rapid moves to 50–75% can undermine the value of a title.

## Decision rule before launch

Keep **$14.99** as the production and revenue-planning assumption. Revisit it only after outside players have used the near-final demo.

Ask testers an open question without presenting price choices:

> Based on what you played, how much would you expect this game to cost on Steam?

Interpret the results alongside behaviour, not in isolation:

- completion rate and hours played;
- whether testers immediately start another run;
- wishlist intent;
- complaints about missing content or unfinished presentation;
- how often testers independently compare it to $20–25 games.

Raise the working price to $17.99 only if the demo repeatedly creates that premium comparison. Reduce it to $12.99 only if the shipped scope remains clearly below the planned three-act release.
