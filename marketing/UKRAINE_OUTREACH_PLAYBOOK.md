# RabbleBound — Ukrainian Outreach Playbook

**Use after reading:** `UKRAINIAN_MARKET_AND_COMMUNITY_RESEARCH.md`  
**Principle:** earn trust through relevance and a good build; do not treat communities as free distribution lists.

## 1. Readiness gates

Do not use calendar dates as the trigger. Use product readiness.

### Gate A — private feedback is safe

All must be true:

- the build launches reliably on the stated Windows configuration;
- the first battle can be reached in five minutes or less;
- a tester can complete a meaningful 20–30 minute path;
- known critical bugs are disclosed;
- the build has a visible version number and a bug-report link;
- every invitation says the current build is in English;
- strings are externalized and Ukrainian font characters have been smoke-tested.

At Gate A, contact only a few exact-fit creators and administrators. Do not make public Discord posts.

### Gate B — public demo is safe

Gate A plus:

- public store/demo page exists;
- trailer or gameplay cut shows the core interaction in the first five seconds;
- audio is streamer-safe;
- save/reset behavior is predictable;
- creator kit has Ukrainian text reviewed by a native speaker;
- language disclosure is visible;
- unique UTM links can be created per outlet/creator;
- there is capacity to answer Ukrainian comments with native help.

At Gate B, approach niche public creators, media, and admin-approved community channels.

### Gate C — broad reach is safe

Gate B plus:

- niche creator tests show qualified interest;
- the first five minutes no longer create recurring confusion;
- capsule, trailer, screenshots, and store description are final-quality;
- the team can absorb a traffic spike and patch quickly;
- any claim about future Ukrainian localization has a real owner, scope, and budget.

At Gate C, approach larger generalist creators and consider paid packages.

## 3. Minimum creator kit

Keep one shared folder or landing page containing:

- one-sentence pitch;
- 80–120 word description;
- current language and localization disclosure;
- Steam/demo link;
- key or build instructions;
- 15–30 second trailer;
- three short unedited gameplay clips;
- six clean screenshots;
- logo and capsule art;
- supported platform, approximate playtime, controls, and known issues;
- content warning, if necessary;
- streamer-safe audio statement;
- contact name and response expectation;
- permission to monetize creator coverage;
- embargo details, only when actually required.

### Recommended one-sentence pitch

> RabbleBound is a dark medieval tactical roguelike where a small, damaged mercenary company fights compact hex battles, chooses its route, and carries every scar and compromise into the next job.

### Comparison line

> Think Battle Brothers combat on a Slay the Spire-style run map, with Wartales-like company growth and stranger character events.

Use comparisons as orientation, never as a claim of endorsement or equivalence.

### Language disclosure

Until localization is contracted:

> The current demo is in English. Ukrainian text is planned for a later stage, but we are not announcing a date until the work is staffed and scheduled.

This sentence should be translated and reviewed by a native Ukrainian speaker before public outreach. Do not machine-translate the entire public pitch without review.

## 7. Structured playtest

### Observe without intervening

Record:

- time to understand movement and attack range;
- time to first meaningful decision;
- first misunderstood icon or tooltip;
- whether flanking/back arcs are discovered;
- whether morale changes are noticed;
- first restart or abandonment point;
- whether the tester voluntarily chooses another node;
- which character is remembered and why.

### Ask after the session

1. In one sentence, what is the game?
2. What decision felt most interesting?
3. What did you think would happen but did not?
4. At what point, if any, did the rules become clear?
5. Did losing health, morale, or a character feel fair?
6. Did any event choice feel like it mattered later?
7. Would you play one more node now? Why or why not?
8. Which comparison—Battle Brothers, Wartales, Slay the Spire, or something else—best explains it?
9. Did the English language create a blocker? Where?
10. What would make you recommend the demo to another tactics player?

Do not ask “Did you like it?” as the primary measure. It produces polite but weak evidence.

## 8. Creator-specific briefing cards

### Borscht & Blade

- Ask about: action economy, formation, enemy intent, meaningful injuries, run pacing.
- Send: raw combat clip and playable build.
- Avoid: lore-heavy deck or reach-focused sponsorship request.
- Success: two or three high-value mechanics corrections, then an authentic first look if the creator wants one.

### zombieVegas UA

- Ask about: tactical clarity, RPG progression, difficulty curve, replay desire.
- Send: complete 20–30 minute route and patch notes.
- Success: specialist feedback and qualified niche traffic.

### Twaryna / HUC

- Ask about: army composition, positioning, morale, map choices, loss recovery.
- Send: a battle that demonstrates a tactical reversal.
- Avoid: assuming all HoMM viewers want a roguelike.
- Success: live strategic discussion and completion of the demo path.

### Ukrainian Geek

- Ask for: first look, stream, or community-approved test.
- Send: public store page, Ukrainian-subtitled short trailer, clean screenshots.
- Success: bridge from niche credibility to broader Ukrainian discovery.

### INRIUM / tabletop creators

- Ask about: character attachment, choice consequences, readable rules, company identity.
- Send: one combat sequence plus one event/scar consequence.
- Avoid: framing the server as an ad audience.
- Success: stronger narrative systems and tabletop crossover interest.

### Ukrainian Localization / KULI ecosystem

- Ask about: font coverage, string context, UI expansion, plural/gender issues, workflow.
- Send: sample export, font/UI screenshots, glossary draft.
- Avoid: an unpaid translation request or a vague “we may localize someday” recruitment post.
- Success: lower later localization cost and fewer architectural mistakes.

### OLDboi / broad media

- Ask for: coverage only when there is a real news beat—public demo, launch, festival, or confirmed localization.
- Send: polished trailer, press kit, accurate dates, store page.
- Success: awareness and wishlists, not design consulting.

### Nika Karuso

- Ask for: nothing until the build reliably produces funny or dramatic stories.
- Send: three self-explanatory moments involving bad mercenaries, ugly choices, or unexpected combat.
- Success: entertainment content that still makes the game understandable.

## 10. Compensation and disclosure

- Ask creators for their current media kit or rate. Do not invent a “Ukrainian market rate.”
- Pay for scheduled consulting, structured written feedback, sponsored coverage, or dedicated production.
- A gifted key alone does not create an obligation to publish.
- Mark paid or sponsored arrangements in the brief and allow the creator to follow platform disclosure rules.
- Compare paid opportunities using cost per qualified demo start, wishlist, or completed session—not follower count alone.
- Do not use affiliate terms that pressure a positive opinion.

## 11. Contact cadence

1. Send one researched message.
2. Wait 7–10 days.
3. Send one concise follow-up with a meaningful update or clearer fit.
4. If there is no answer, close the contact and revisit only at a major new milestone.

Never pursue a creator across email, Discord, Telegram, and personal social accounts simultaneously.

## 12. Ukrainian-language rollout checklist

Before announcing full localization:

- [ ] Translation scope and word count estimated
- [ ] Translator/team contracted
- [ ] Glossary owner assigned
- [ ] All player-facing strings exportable
- [ ] Ukrainian glyph/font test passed
- [ ] UI expansion test passed
- [ ] Plural/gender/context support tested
- [ ] Screenshots available to translators
- [ ] Linguistic QA build scheduled
- [ ] Store assets and subtitles scoped
- [ ] Patch/update translation budget allocated
- [ ] Announcement date approved by the localization owner

## 13. Stop conditions

Pause Ukrainian promotion if:

- recurring testers cannot understand the first battle without live help;
- the build is unstable enough to waste creator time;
- public messaging implies localization is confirmed when it is not;
- the team cannot respond to native-language questions;
- a community administrator has not granted permission;
- the only justification is a large follower number with no genre fit.

Resume when the specific condition is fixed, not after an arbitrary delay.
