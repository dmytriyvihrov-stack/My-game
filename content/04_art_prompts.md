# Art prompts — painted, per the locked direction

**Direction: PAINTED illustration** (decided; see concept §14 and the addendum atop
`02_ART_DIRECTION.md`). The pixel-art specs are superseded.

Every prompt below assumes this **style block** — paste it into each one:

> *Painted digital illustration, dark low-fantasy, desaturated earth palette — mud brown, bone
> grey, wet slate, dull ochre. Cold overcast light from one direction. Heavy atmospheric haze,
> distance fading to pale grey-blue. Grounded and dirty, no heroism, no bright colour except
> where noted. Build the picture through a readable silhouette, one clear gesture line and large
> connected value masses. Use confident directional strokes and deliberate quiet areas; add only
> selective detail that follows form or tells the story. No pointillist AI noise, random speckles,
> all-over scratches or uniform micro-texture. In the spirit of Battle Brothers and The Banner Saga.*

**Line before noise:** review every asset at its actual in-game size. If a mark disappears in the
live crop, remove it instead of sharpening it. Human-made character comes from chosen line weight,
connected shapes, asymmetry and omission, not from filling every surface with dots. For battlefield
tokens, species, weapon, posture and one emotion must remain readable at 32–48 px. Stop there when
those facts read; the player's imagination should complete the unstated detail.

**Side before ornament:** for battlefield humans, ratkin, ogres and other faction units, place one
broad blue or teal garment layer on allies and one broad rust or red garment layer on enemies. It
should cover roughly a quarter to a third of the visible body and may be a different cloak, tabard,
scarf, sash or coat panel on each role. Do not rely on tiny trim, badges, outlines, weapons or eye
colour to communicate allegiance. Unique ununiformed beasts keep natural colour.

**Size roughly signals danger:** review battlefield characters beside one another, not in isolation.
Ordinary dogs must read smaller than ratkin, ratkin smaller than humans, and humans smaller than
ogres and major armoured threats. Use a deceptive exception only when the encounter calls for it.

**Copy into every future battlefield generation prompt:**

> *Use one shared ground line, camera and distance for every unit. Preserve the visible body-size
> hierarchy: dogs smaller than ratkin, ratkin smaller than humans, humans smaller than ogres, and
> major armoured threats at least as imposing as ogres. Do not enlarge each subject separately to
> fill its atlas cell. Leave more empty space around smaller bodies. The final pack will use display
> multipliers of dogs 75 percent, ratkin 85 percent, humans 105 percent, ogres 100 percent, and
> Something in armour 114 percent. Review the complete pack together at actual 1x battlefield size.*

**The saturation rule still governs everything:** if it is saturated, it is magical or dangerous.

The four atmosphere keys from the Style Bible are the grading:
- **3a Fogbound Teal** — battles, ambushes, anything outnumbered *(default)*
- **3b Dust & Gold** — the map, holdings, contracts, anywhere you stop
- **3c The Bloom** — the corrupted region only. Magenta air, no visible source.
- **3d Stolen Daylight** — rationed to two or three screens in the whole act

## Priority 1 — character portraits (the roster needs faces)

Format: **chest-up, three-quarter view, dark vignette background**, so they drop straight into
the character sheet.

**`P1` The Captain (you)**
> A human mercenary captain, chest-up, three-quarter view. Late thirties, tired, competent.
> Ordinary ugly — broken nose, bad shave, one eyebrow scarred through. Good armour that has been
> repaired badly: a decent mail hauberk with a mismatched pauldron. A small banner-crest at the
> shoulder, faded. He is not posing. Dust & Gold grading.

**`P2` Vesna Kolb — human spearwoman**
> A woman in her forties, chest-up, three-quarter. Weathered, thin-lipped, entirely unbothered.
> Grey coming in at the temple. Patched gambeson, spear haft visible over the shoulder. The face
> of somebody who has buried three husbands and does not discuss it. Fogbound Teal grading.

**`P3` Skree — ratkin cutter**
> A ratkin: humanoid rat, chest-up, three-quarter. Grey-pink patchy fur, long naked snout,
> yellowed incisors, one notched ear, wet black darting eyes. Scavenged leather hood, rags bound
> with cord, small stolen trinkets sewn into the cloth. Verminous and clever and oddly
> sympathetic — not cartoonish, not cute. Fogbound Teal grading.

**`P4` Bruht — ogre brute**
> An enormous ogre, chest-up, shoulders overflowing the frame so the head looks small on top of
> them. Mottled grey-green hide, heavy brow, deep-set dull eyes, blunt tusks, old rope scars at
> the neck. Scrap plate lashed over bare skin with rope — armour clearly made for something
> smaller. Slow, sad, dangerous. Not a monster: a person.

**`P5` Marrow — human battle-mage**
> A thin human man, chest-up, three-quarter, mid-fifties. Ink-stained fingers, a short sword at
> the hip and a plain rod at his back — he is visibly a swordsman who also reads. Sunken eyes.
> One faint magenta reflection catching in the pupil and nowhere else in the frame.

**`P6–P8` Recruit portrait set** — generate three more each of: generic human, generic ratkin,
generic ogre. Same framing, no distinguishing gear, so they can be assigned to rolled recruits.

## Priority 2 — event scenes (16:9, painted, no text, no UI)

**`E1` The Hanged Toll-Man** *(reference event)*
> A muddy stone bridge over dead marshland under a heavy overcast sky. A hanged man in good
> boots turning slowly from a gibbet at the bridge-head. Crows. A ruined watchtower on the
> horizon. Dust & Gold. Melancholy, not gruesome — the boots are the most detailed thing in
> the frame.

**`E2` The Fen-Mother**
> A vast six-legged beast the size of a hay-wain, lying dead-still in bloom-lit marsh, mistakable
> for a fallen barn. A head like a split anvil. Magenta light running under the hide in slow
> bands. A dog-sized cub behind her, half-hidden. The Bloom grading — magenta is the ONLY
> saturated colour, all greens gone near-black. Quietly wrong rather than dramatic.

**`E3` The Dead Company**
> Nine dead mercenaries in a roadside ditch, four or five days gone, still in good harness.
> Seen from the road looking down. Their gear is untouched; the food and boots are missing.
> Fogbound Teal, heavy fog, no gore in focus — the wrongness is that the armour is better than
> yours.

**`E4` The Muster Field**
> A low stone wall at a crossroads under a wide pale sky, with three ragged strangers of
> different races sitting on it waiting to be hired. A banner-pole with a faded pennant. Dust &
> Gold. This is a hopeful frame — one of very few.

**`E5` The Ratkin Snare**
> A road strung with small bells on cord across dead marsh at dusk. A boar-hauling cage at the
> far end with a human woman folded inside it. Ratkin shapes in the reeds, indistinct. A large
> one under a crude standard. Fogbound Teal, tense, outnumbered.

**`E6` Under the Bloom**
> Forest interior washed in unnatural magenta and violet light with NO visible source. Moss and
> foliage gone near-black beneath it. Bare crooked trees, mist in the middle distance, ruined
> stone half-swallowed. Nothing glows — the air itself is the wrong colour.

**`E7` The Last Ship**
> A human sailing vessel sixty years wrecked, far up a grey shingle beach, impossibly distant
> from any water. Weathered to bone. A firepit inside the hull. Stolen Daylight grading — real
> sky, real light — used here deliberately, because this is where the humans came from.

**`E8` The Clan Cart** · **`E9` The Sitting Stone** · **`E10` The Collector**
> Follow the same recipe: one clear subject, Dust & Gold, one human-scale detail that carries
> the joke or the sadness (eleven hundred shoddy crossbows / an ogre who has sat two winters /
> a ledger and a stamped writ for a kingdom that fell).

## Priority 3 — creatures and factions

**`C1` Ratkin rank-and-file** — three variants: spear, cleaver, slinger. Mangy, improvised kit.
**`C2` Ratkin chieftain** — bigger, better armoured, a standard, visibly in charge.
**`C3` Ratkin warp-sniffer** — small, unarmed, a staff, magenta light at the tip.
**`C4` The Broken Men** — five human deserters, half a uniform each, a captain who still stands
like one. *They should read as sympathetic.* That is the whole point of them.
**`C5` The cub** — dog-sized version of the Fen-Mother, outsized head, no sense.

---

## How to use these

1. Generate **P1–P5 first**. The roster is what the player looks at most.
2. Then **E1, E2, E5** — the three events that carry the act.
3. Judge every result against one question: **does it look like the same world as the last one?**
   If a piece is prettier but doesn't match, it is a worse piece.
4. Bring back what missed and why — the style block gets refined from real results, not theory.
