How to read this

Every word below is the game’s own. The bodies, the door labels, the sub-lines and the outcomes are read out of EVENTS, CAMPS, VIGNETTES and MERCY in prototype/grimtoll_slice.html, so nothing here is a copy that can go stale. Where a card’s text is a function of the run, every branch it can take is printed and labelled.

Names are stood in for: [A] and [B] are the two roster members a fire card is about, and the bracketed phrases are the game’s own cast tokens ([your ogre], [the ratkin at the fire], and so on).

The highlights are a reading, not a verdict:

AMBERthe whole card is weak: no decision, no risk, or a fork that is not really a fork
PINKone door is dead: dominated by a sibling, or costs and pays nothing
BLUEa thread the card opens that nothing in the build ever closes
GREENkept as the benchmark — this is the shape the flagged cards should be held against

The three findings worth acting on first

  1. A race door is free and pays more than the door beside it. On A WEDDING ON THE ROAD, SOMETHING IN THE PORK, THE SITTING STONE and WHAT THE PEAT KEPT, the needRace door costs nothing and pays more than the door that costs food or a day. Since the game only ever shows ONE race door and the ratkin’s wins, a company holding a ratkin resolves all four cards to a free maximum. The fix is not to cut the race doors — they are some of the best writing on the road — it is that they should cost something.
  2. rep: is dead data on seven doors, and a live rule still reads it. Factions were cut, and the applier says so in a comment (“c.rep fields are inert”). But doorGives() and doorCosts() — the pair that decides whether a walk-away door gets deleted from a card — still count rep as a payment. So seven doors advertise a payoff that does not exist, and one of the build’s own content rules is reading a field that pays nothing.
  3. Eight cards end on a promise nothing keeps. The salt-wives walking inland, the clan that now knows your banner, the drowned village, the name carved by the bunk. Every one of them is a good last line, and every one of them is the end of the thread. Compare WHAT THEY DID BEFORE, which is the only fire card that writes a node onto the world map.

Appendix — how this was checked

The dominated-door list is not a reading. It compares every pair of doors on a card across crowns, provisions, salvage, gems, morale and days, and reports a door that is worse or equal on every axis.

It refuses to compare a door carrying anything it cannot price, and that exclusion is the whole reason the list is short. The first run of it reported eleven road cards; six of those were doors carrying healAll (the shrine), blessAll (beside it), opensRoad (the sinkhole), strandOne (next to it), the two plantStash modes on WHAT THEY DID BEFORE, and needUnpaid on the long fire — all real payloads that live nowhere in fx, and all working exactly as designed. It also refuses to compare two doors that sit on disjoint sin branches, because those are never both on screen.

What survives that filter is in the door notes above. The rep: finding came out of the same pass: four of the doors it cleared were only cleared because they carry a rep field, and reading the applier showed that field pays nothing.
