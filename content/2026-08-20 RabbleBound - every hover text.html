RabbleBound — every hover text

Every string the game shows on hover, grouped by the screen it belongs to. Generated 2026-08-20 from the shipped build.

How to read this

Each entry says WHERE it belongs before it gives the text, so a line can be found on screen without hunting for it.

There are two different hover systems in this build and it matters which one a line is on:

  • The browser tooltip — a title= attribute. Plain text only, no bold, no colour, and it waits about a second before it appears. 43 of the player-facing hover sites are on this one, and several of them build a different string per body or per hex.
  • The game’s own tooltip box (#gtTip) — styled, instant, follows the cursor, and can hold a heading, a coloured ladder and a footnote. 23 hover sites use this, and they carry most of the words.

Counted over the whole build: 83 hover sites, of which 17 are behind DEV.MODE (section 10) and 66 are player-facing.

Values in {braces} are filled in at the moment of the hover. {G.run.crowns} is what is in the chest right now; {ms.n} is whichever mood the company is in. They are shown as braces here so the sentence around them is readable.

Where a hover is derived rather than authored — built out of the item's or the act's own numbers, so it cannot drift from them — the template is given, then the table it reads from.

3 · The world bar

The strip of chips across the top of the map. These carry the most words of any hover in the game.

Fires as the game’s own tooltip box (#gtTip): styled, instant, and it follows the cursor. Each one is a heading, a body, sometimes an italic footnote, and sometimes a gold ▸ CLICK: line saying what pressing it does.

THE COMPANY — the left-hand chip with the portrait on it

★ {lvUp} unspent level. Somebody has earned a perk or a stat point and nobody has chosen it yet. It waits on their sheet until you do.

How many bodies the wagon and the pay-chest can carry. Everybody takes room by size - ratkin 1, human 2, ogre 3 - so a fistful of ratkin and one ogre cost the same seats. Widen the wagon bed at a wheelwright to raise it, permanently.

▸ CLICK: the roster, their sheets and their gear.

THE KIT — the leather sack chip beside it

The wagon's stash, and what everybody is wearing out of it.

▸ CLICK: the same screen, straight to work.

PAYDAY — the PAYDAY chip

Wages are paid every {num()} days, not daily. The bill grows with every day of road - ratkin 1, human 2, ogre 2, and the Captain draws one - and on the day, the chest opens.

Run up so far: ◉{owedNow}. Due in {num()} day: about ◉{pBill}, against {G.run.crowns} in the chest. That bill does not know what the road is about to charge you.

It never stops you taking a road. Owing them is a way a run can go.

UNPAID — the red UNPAID chip, which only appears when wages are behind

They counted, and they said nothing.

Every day the chest cannot pay drags the company's mood down hard, and costs nerve on the field - the personalities disagree about how loudly.

Fill the chest and the wages catch up on their own at day's end.

{ms.ic} {ms.n} — the face chip — the company’s mood

Every fight starts closer to this mood than to steady.

▸ CLICK: throw them a party - {feastCost()} provisions.

{hs.n} · {hs.pct}% — the heart chip — the company’s health

Days on the road are the only medicine in this game.

▸ CLICK: double rations - {RATION_COST} provisions, and they mend twice as fast for {num()} days.

CROWNS — the ◉ crowns segment

The purse of THIS run - wages, tolls, hires, bribes. It does not survive the company. Spend it like somebody who knows that.

Wages fall due every fourth day, walking or sitting.

PROVISIONS - THE GOOD BARRELS — the ❦ provisions segment

Your wages already buy the company bread. This is everything better than bread: salt meat, cheese, a jug.

Nothing eats them on its own - not a day on the road, not a wound mending. Every one you lose, you spent.
Double rations - wounds mend twice as fast for three days.
Lay out a proper meal - the mood lifts hard (once a day).
The road asks for it - feeding a stranger, a wedding, a hungry ogre.

#157 took the last automatic drain off them: the wounded used to eat one a day whether you liked it or not.

SALVAGE — the ▤ salvage segment

Timber, iron and fittings pulled off the road. It builds things INTO THE WAGON between runs - beds, chests, fires - and pays village craftsmen.

Survives the run. Everything else dies with the company.

GEMS — the ◈ gems segment

Stones the wrong colour for this country. The one thing both worlds take: spend them in-run in a pinch, or bank them for the wagon.

The deliberate bridge between the purse and the future.

pet — the animal chip, when the company has one

Assigned to the Captain. Check the company screen to change or remove it.

THE CONTRACT — the contract plaque

A man of the lord's holds Skelbrook, at the far end of the valley: one village, one hall, bandits in it since the thaw. He has been writing to the lord every week since. Bring back the head out of the hall's chair. Keep the purse.

It pays on delivery, and not before.

Worth a look. WHERE: the class mark on the sheet, the muster field and the battle plaque

Fires as the game’s own tooltip box (#gtTip): styled, instant, and it follows the cursor. Prose, then an italic second line about how it plays, then — for somebody you have actually hired — the acts on their bar right now and the perk tree above them.

CAPTAIN

You. On the field, in reach, and worth killing, which is the point: leadership that costs nothing is not leadership.

COMMAND steadies the line, HOLD THE LINE hardens it. If the Captain falls the whole company takes the shock, and he is the only one who is never left behind.

SPEARWOMAN

The line. Reach 2 means she fights over the person in front of her, which is what turns a row of bodies into a wall.

With a boar spear her signature is SPEAR WALL - one action, and anything stepping into reach takes 75% of it and is often shoved back out. It stops dead the moment somebody gets inside her reach, so she needs a front rank. With a HALBERD it is BRACE AND SHOVE instead: the weapon decides the job.

ARCHER

Useless in a scrum and decisive from four hexes, so the rest of the line exists partly to keep the scrum off her.

CRIPPLING SHOT does not kill the thing running at you. It takes its legs. Her off hand is a real decision: a quiver, or a dirk for when something reaches her anyway.

BATTLE-MAGE

A mediocre swordsman who occasionally does something appalling. One school, one working a turn, and a free hand for a blade - low magic written as a class.

Casting costs his own nerve, and a working is aimed with the INTELLECT, so a stupid one misses. What he HOLDS decides how far a working carries: a working stave adds 2 hexes to every school, a sword adds none and is a great deal better in the scrum.

MAGE

Two schools and a second working each turn at half strength, and almost nothing else. Bad with a blade, bad at dodging, thin. The line exists to keep him alive.

Deliberately worse in melee than the battle-mage: the best offence in the company should not also be adequate with a sword.

CUTTER

Not a damage dealer. A SETUP piece: he makes the opening and the whole line takes it, including him.

DISTRACT turns a target to face away from him: its back is then open to everybody, at +30 to hit and +25% damage. He also reads the ground: +1 hex on his first move.

BRUTE

The answer to being surrounded is one horizontal reply through all of it.

SWEEP hits every adjacent enemy at 75%. ROAR takes 28 morale off everything within two hexes. Wants to be where the crowd is, which is the opposite of everybody else.

Appendix — what is NOT on a hover

Two things came up while collecting this and are worth writing down.

Nothing on the world bar’s resource segments says what a number will be tomorrow except PAYDAY. Crowns, provisions, salvage and gems each explain what the resource IS, at length and well; only the payday chip tells you what is about to be taken off you.

The two hover systems are not used consistently. The company sheet and the world bar are almost entirely on the game’s own tooltip box, where a line can be bold, coloured and instant. The battle screen — where the player has the least time and the most to read — is almost entirely on the browser tooltip, which is plain text and waits a second before appearing. Every status badge, every formation badge, the order rail and the zone-of-control warning are on the slow one.
